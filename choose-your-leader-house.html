For instructors

Choose Your Leader is a situational judgment exercise. It records where you went each night, what you said at dinner and to whom, what you needed to know, and whether you went where the answer was. It reports those as a description, not a score, and it does not claim to measure or predict leadership ability.

One house, one week, five evenings. Every night opens on the set, because the news was the one aperture every household shared; then one room, and that is the rule, and the rule is the argument: nobody had the whole picture. At dinner the player names what they need to know, from what the two rooms raised; the answer waits in a room on a later night and is shown only if the player goes there. No door ever signals where the answer is. The words at dinner, the questions and the neighbor’s lines are drawn from pools each play, so a second walk through the week is not the first. Nothing is remembered between visits. Every item is dated and attributed; lines marked VERIFY await the sourcing pass. Under the paper in the kitchen, one dated item a night from the policy-gap dossier: the decision, whose table it landed on, and the source. Three items that were not public that week appear only in the replay, each with the date it became public. The kitchen moves one step farther from the set on every night the sheet stays folded.

Critical Thinking artifact assessment, 1-page reflection.
--Submitted by Matt Walsh

Hi Tom and Sean,

Thanks for having me be a part of this process. I've got a few significant takeaways, but first of all, you've done a stellar job communicating about the project, conducting the norming, and coordinating the online scoring process. I don't have any recommendations to make in the execution of the assessment.

I've spent so much time assessing composition from a specific framework that it's very engaging, actually, to apply some of the same processes I'm familiar with to scoring specific assignments from different disciplines.

The English Department and Assessment Coordinator have discussed scoring portfolios with the corresponding assignments, and while we have this for the exit assessment exam itrself, we do not currently collect instructor assignments. I'm not 100% sure that we need to review them the way we currently operate, but I also see through this model that the holistic model of assessment that we use doesn't capture important information about specific learning objectives, and I think we can do better,

I'd like to recommend that the English Department and Placement Coordinator develop a set of accurately measurable outcomes that our portfolio scoring process could assess, similar to this process. We'd research and debate and prioritize the outcomes we want to assess.

We should be measuring, and asking for in our assignments and portfolio process, high level outcomes such as: evidence of analytical reading, interpretation, engagement with the text, synthesis; using rhetorical strategies to develop, advance, and conclude an argument; considering the needs of the audience; supporting a central claim with relevant, credible evidence, etc.

Information on specific outcomes would help provide insight into the significant distinction, for example, between a 3 placement (EN98) and a 4 placement (EN101). That's all we currently record in our portfolio process, the final placement, read by two or three readers. I think that we do it well and it's a valuable process for students and the participants, but we currently collect no data about areas of student strength or weakness, which specific skills students excel at and struggle with, and therefore we have insufficient means to review our curriculum. Being part of this process helped provide me with that valuable perspective.

Thanks,
—Matt

## Why this is on file (studio relevance)

This reflection is the **primary-source rationale for the measure side of the iSLO suite**, in the founder's
own words:

- **The core finding** — *"the holistic model of assessment that we use doesn't capture important information
  about specific learning objectives … we currently collect no data about areas of student strength or
  weakness, which specific skills students excel at and struggle with, and therefore we have insufficient
  means to review our curriculum."* That is exactly the gap **`close-the-loop.html`** is built to close: it
  records results **per rubric dimension**, not just a single holistic score, so the report can say *where*
  students are strong and weak.
- **The named opportunity** — develop **accurately measurable outcomes for the EN placement portfolio**
  (currently only the final placement, EN98 vs EN101, is recorded; two–three readers; no skill-level data).
  The listed candidate outcomes — analytical reading, interpretation, engagement, synthesis; rhetorical
  strategies to develop/advance/conclude an argument; audience awareness; supporting a central claim with
  credible evidence — are a ready-made draft rubric. A natural **next measure-side build**: an
  EN-placement-portfolio scoring instrument (norming + per-outcome capture) modeled on The Norming Table,
  turning "placement 3 vs 4" into skill-level data the department can review curriculum against.
- **On execution** — Matt had *no* recommendations for how Tom and Sean ran the norming / online scoring; the
  recommendation is entirely about **what gets measured**, not how the scoring was run.

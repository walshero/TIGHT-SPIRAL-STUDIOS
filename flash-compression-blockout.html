Six cards. One frame. Ground and light are the only things that vary.

Look at the six scenes

Stage 0 blockout, not the game and not a skin. Nothing here is textured and nothing here is precious. A rejected blockout costs minutes. A rejected finished skin costs a day, and the studio has paid the day version twice. Take any scene over and it becomes yours. The chair is open.

Two kinds of card, marked by shape. A round chip with one dot means the move fires once per piece. A square chip with three dots means it stacks. Shape and label both carry it, so the distinction survives colourblindness and warm-dark.

The six

One per piece
First Domino
The first thing that tips, that makes everything after it fall.

The tipped tile is the brightest thing in the frame and the rank darkens as it goes back, so the eye reads cause before it reads row. Depth is three values, not a filter.

Matt's card · singular · set live 2026-06-29

One per piece
Start After the Storm
Begin when everything but the action is over. The reader walks in mid-consequence.

The light source is offstage. The door is the brightest shape and the event that made it sits outside the frame. That is the whole card, drawn: you never see the storm, you see the wedge it threw across the floor.

Bought in · singular · Matt's theatre principle

Many per piece
World Detail That Matters
A concrete detail doing real work. Not a token, not a totem.

Four objects, one lit. The card is not "put objects in." It is which one gets the light. The dim three are why the lit one reads as chosen.

Matt's card · plural · title held plain, better metaphor pending

Many per piece
Interior Weather
The felt state made visible. The image holds the feeling so you never state it.

The figure is the smallest and darkest thing. The weather is the biggest. The composition argues the card before a word does: the state is around the person, not announced by them.

Many per piece
Backstory Snap
History implied in a flash, not delivered. The reader infers the weight.

One frame lit out of four, and the dashed edge says it was taken, not screened. The three dark frames are the history you are not going to show.

Matt's card · plural

Many per piece
Slide
A shift in status, distance, or relation that moves the piece without announcing it.

Two planes, same size, one displaced. The ticks below are the only thing that says a move happened, which is the card: the reader measures it, the narrator never mentions it.

Matt's card · plural · example still owed in Matt's voice

The published model

"Palate Cleanser," Michelle Ross, 2019

366 words. Assigned reading per the EN195 syllabus in two different positions: the 6-week online section reads it in the flash unit beside Kincaid's "Girl" and García Márquez's "One of These Days," and the 15-week section reads it early, in class, beside the Secret World assignment. A mother tells her grown daughter about a postcard.

Four of the six cards are in it, and a reader can find them. Short anchors only. The story is the students' to read whole.

Interior Weather · the pivot
Why this story earns the model slot. The two singular cards fire in the first two sentences and never fire again, which is what "one per piece" means, shown rather than asserted. And the ending does something no card yet covers: the same sorbet that freed the mother is what the daughter loses when the one soufflé arrives to be shared. That may be a seventh card, or it may be the reason the deck is not locked. Founder call.

This is a model, not the neutral draft. The spec reserves the playable draft for Matt's own hand: not a published story, not a student's, not a machine's.

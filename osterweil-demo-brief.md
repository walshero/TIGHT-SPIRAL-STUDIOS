## 2. His life work, verified

**Before games: theater and television.** Osterweil's artistic background was
mainly theater and TV — he came to game design as a storyteller and visual
designer, arriving at design sessions "with maps, visuals, and storylines floating
around his head" (TERC's own Zoombinis history). He is a scene-first designer by
formation, not a mechanics-first one.

**Zoombinis (TERC, 1996).** Co-created with Chris Hancock: *Logical Journey of the
Zoombinis*, math and logic through pure play — half a million copies, two sequels,
the defining counterexample to drill-and-kill edutainment. The companion essay
"Zoombinis and the Art of Mathematical Play" (Hancock & Osterweil) is the primary
statement of his method: the mathematics IS the play, never chocolate on broccoli.

**MIT Education Arcade.** Creative Director; research director in MIT Comparative
Media Studies/Writing. Designs there: *Vanished* (MIT/Smithsonian curated mystery,
environmental science), *Lure of the Labyrinth* (math), *Kids Survey Network*
(data), *Caduceus* (medicine), *iCue* (history). Public stance (Slate, 2015):
games, **not gamification** — points and badges bolted onto content are the
opposite of his project.

**Learning Games Network.** Founder, President, and Creative Director of the
Education Arcade's nonprofit spinoff — the bridge between research and practice.
There he led the Gates Foundation's Language Learning Initiative (ESL) — **the
program Xenos-ISLE and Matt's residency lived inside** — and designed *Quandary*
(2013 Games for Change Game of the Year): ethical thinking for kids 8-14, built on
perspective-taking, prediction, and consequences. His words on it: *"We wanted a
game to get the player to understand that in any situation there are multiple
points of view."* Quandary's core loop scores the player on **predicting how other
characters will react** — and it deliberately has **no right answers**, only
consequences; it frames ethical decision-making without telling players what to
think.

**The Four Freedoms of Play** — his signature framework, already the studio's
spine (OS §1, tight-spiral-runbook FOUR FREEDOMS GUARD):
1. **Freedom to fail** — mistakes without serious consequences;
2. **Freedom to experiment** — invent new approaches;
3. **Freedom to try on identities**;
4. **Freedom of effort** — alternate intense and relaxed engagement.
To Osterweil these are also the four freedoms of *learning*: environments that
lack them don't teach well.

**Resonant Games (MIT Press, 2018** — with Klopfer, Haas, Rosenheck). Twenty
design principles under four "honors": honor the **whole learner** (a full human
being, not an empty vessel); honor the **sociality** of learning and play; honor
a **deep connection between the content and the game**; honor the **learning
context** (usually the public school classroom). The book's thesis is the
studio's thesis: integrate content and play instead of wrapping curriculum in
game-skin.

**Also:** co-author credit across learning-games research; play consultant on the
Emmy-winning Amazon series *Tumble Leaf*.

## 3. What his lens means for the CYL demo

Where CYL already speaks his language — say it plainly, in his vocabulary:

- **Content-mechanic identity (his deepest principle).** CYL's lesson IS its
  mechanic: blind commit → the record turns → feel the distance. Nothing is
  wrapper. This is the Zoombinis test and CYL passes it; lead with that.
- **Quandary kinship (the strongest bridge).** Quandary: no right answers,
  perspective-taking, prediction, consequences-not-verdicts. CYL: the win is
  *calibration, not cynicism*; the Lincoln no-gap scene rewards noticing there's
  nothing to distrust; the three-homes design is perspective-taking made
  architectural — the same October, three media apertures. He built the game
  closest to this one. He will see the kinship in minutes if the demo lets him.
- **Freedom to fail.** The three trauma rails are this freedom, engineered: the
  consequence lands on the rhetoric's reach, never the player's worth; the brake
  is always live; the deep ending teaches, never ambushes. Landing at the floor
  while naming the gap *is still the win* — failure without punishment.
- **No gamification anywhere.** No points, badges, streaks; the reveal is the
  reward (standing studio rule). He has spent a decade attacking gamification in
  public; CYL's cleanliness here is a feature to make visible, not assume.
- **Whole learner.** Comfort corner, accessibility floor, scene-first opening,
  one ask per screen — honor-the-whole-learner in practice.
- **Glass Engine.** The end-of-game depth rings showing the instrument itself —
  research-lab honesty he'll recognize as Education Arcade culture.

Where he may press — be ready, don't pre-hedge:

- **Freedom of effort in a linear scene chain.** CYL's scene sequence is fixed
  and each scene demands the same intensity. Where can a player coast, wander,
  or replay? (The Listings Page chapter-select and Second Hearing partially
  answer; the honest answer is "v1 is tight; the freedoms widen in the full
  game.")
- **Freedom to experiment vs. the measurement firewall.** Horvath's instrument
  discipline deliberately narrows player choice at the two asks. Osterweil may
  read that as play constrained by psychometrics. The defensible frame: the
  constraint is one screen out of many, and it is what makes the noticing
  *visible to the player themselves* — the reveal his own games run on.
- **Sociality.** CYL v1 is single-player and silent about the classroom around
  it. His Resonant lens will ask how two players compare descents. (Confluence
  rater-norming is the studio's real answer at the assessment layer.)

## 4. The demo posture (what the research says about HOW, not just what)

He is a designer formed by theater who builds games where playing IS the
argument. So: **hand him the game, not a deck.** The pending producer call
(HITL-REVIEW, Rep's advocacy) already points at `choose-your-leader-v5-slice.html`
— one scene, ~4 minutes, the shortest path to the mechanic's payoff — and this
research independently supports that pick: every game he's shipped earns its
theory *after* play, never before. Let him land in the 1962 room cold, exactly as
a player would. The Xenos-ISLE / LGN / four-freedoms lineage is the conversation
*after* he plays, and it's a homecoming, not a citation.

**Which build is still the founder's call** (HITL-REVIEW producer decision,
unchanged by this brief): v5-slice first per the Rep; v5 if he bites; v6
alongside only if his interest turns to the assessment instrument.

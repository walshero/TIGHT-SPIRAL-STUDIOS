    - paragraph: The second trip was different. What possessed us to do the things we did? We went to Ellie and Billy's old house up the road, the one they had rented. We took the mail out of the mailbox and wrote on it, "You're dead meat, Ed," and put it back in the box. The door had been unlocked. I remember pouring myself a glass of orange juice from the fridge and leaving the glass on the table.
    - paragraph: After we let ourselves out of the stranger's house, we wandered into the backwoods, past the trail and beyond the cabin. We found an abandoned school bus that must've been someone's home, once. We went through everything, photographs of strangers in their special moments. Heaps of clothes and trinkets. As we left, we broke a bottle of champagne across the headlights.
    - paragraph: "\"We christened it,\" you later told my uncle. Why'd you tell him, I said with my face."
    - paragraph: Which brings us to the ants.
    - paragraph: We slept in sleeping bags on the plywood floor of the attic turret, an octagon with windows, the tops of trees, and moonlight. The turret had been framed in 1979 with pine boards my uncle bought from a lumberyard in Charlemont, and the windows were double-hung, the kind that stick in summer, with brass latches my aunt found at a flea market in Brattleboro. [noticed] We did that thing with my aunt and uncle's liquor cabinet, drained off the vodka and the rum and topped the bottles up with water. Not enough to get crazy drunk, like we did at home in the Arboretum, but enough to catch a good buzz.
    - paragraph: There were ants in the attic, I remember. It was like an invasion, and we squished and smeared and annihilated dozens after dozens after dozens. And then you started talking about regret, all drunk philosophical, for the times you never told a girl that you liked her, for the times you didn't stand up to an authority figure in the wrong. And for killing all those ants. The sanctity of all life, or something like that, and I remember that you were genuinely sad. You made a promise to never kill another creature ever, big or small.
    - paragraph: "[look here] Then we were standing by the payphones across from Sparr's, waiting for the 39, the same place where I'd stood with the football team after practice when we found out that Magic Johnson had contracted AIDS, and I saw you. An ant on the sidewalk, and you stepped without thinking, slid your sneaker across the pavement and smeared it from existence, and then you looked at me to see if I had seen."

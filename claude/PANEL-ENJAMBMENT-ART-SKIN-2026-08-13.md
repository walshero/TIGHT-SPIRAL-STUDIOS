## THE DISCUSSION

**Creative Director (the spine, and the actual finding).** The skin is not merely
unsatisfying, it is *contradicting the game*. Every mechanic in ENJAMBMENT is
industrial: a belt that will not stop wanting more, a guillotine you arm, a piston that
stomps the meter, an engine that drives the line, lamps that count it. The skin is
pastoral — woods, moon, snow. The player is operating heavy machinery in a forest. The
founder's own originating image was Lucy and Ethel on the chocolate line, and it is the
one image in the brief that has never been honored. The machinery is already built; it
is standing in the wrong room.

**Reads-As Legibility (HALT).** Whatever wins, the moving poem stays the brightest,
highest-contrast thing on screen — it is the text the player is reading *and* the
control they are tapping. That single constraint eliminates full Times Square neon
(competing light sources directly behind moving type) and eliminates busy Samorost
texture behind the ticker. Industrial surfaces are low-chroma, matte, and recede; they
are the friendliest possible ground for glowing type.

**Technical Artist (HALT).** Any skin must survive four things already true of this
file: still-mode, where the stage grows to the full height of the passage; the reader's
largest base font; motion stop; and the rAF belt, which must not pay per-frame cost for
scenery. Note the counter-intuitive consequence — **a photographic plate is cheaper
here than the animated CSS scene it would replace**, because it is static. The current
woods cost more to run than a real photograph would.

**Concept Artist (the process grievance, entered against the session, not a seat).**
The woods were built with no blockout and no sign-off. That is exactly the
expensive-rework spiral this seat exists to prevent, and it has now cost a full skin.
The correction is procedural and binds the next pass: **rough first, signed, then
built.** Accordingly this panel ships blockouts, not a skin.

**Period Librarian (the Rep's advocacy seat).** The lane nobody named, and the only one
that clears both gates at once. Public-domain industrial and assembly-line photography
is abundant and verifiable: Lewis Hine's factory work, FSA/OWI and WPA-era industrial
photography, NARA holdings, and period Times Square zipper photographs. Every mount
records source, license, and the URL it was verified at. This is also the seat that can
finally clear SUB-50-TABLEAU, because `one-thing-gate` counts only real image
elements — a CSS scene is invisible to it no matter how good it looks.

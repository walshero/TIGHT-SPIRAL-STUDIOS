## 1. Playtest log

Sessions on v0.8.1: A attentive, default week; B sloppy, all-stress week, failing on purpose; C all-stress, final synapse, no steering.

- **0-3 s, warm-up.** Tube already humming, good. A node toast arrives before I touch anything, with the steer hint on top of it (A-01).
- **Warm-up pickups.** Green says "Bad bands narrower"; the warm-up has no bands (A-02).
- **Tray.** Paragraph plus four-line legend; cubes start below the fold (A-03). The live spine preview is the best teaching object in the game: all red drops it to two (B-03b). This was the only place I understood "stress means fewer dots."
- **13 s.** "Short night" pops up untouched; speed falls 75 to 17 (A-effect1). Punished for nothing I did.
- **Checkpoint replays.** Every checkpoint stops play for two slowed clips, about 40 s of a 135 s ride in session A. Captions say short nights "thin the landing places"; I never saw one change.
- **Synapses 1-3.** The spine face is a thumbnail until the last half-second (A-syn0). A flash, then a toast about Cajal's drawing (A-landed0). No jump, nothing crossed a gap. "Was that the synapse?"
- **Miss.** Rewind 9 units, "Line up with a lit spine." Sloppy, I missed a 5-spine face twice (B-miss2). It felt like failing an aim test.
- **Fade (B).** Calm sheet, instant return; then the same red card fired again, bands still at maximum.
- **Final synapse, stressed (C).** The pull pinned me to the alarm side, which has no spines: 11 misses in 20 s, no end. Steering against it landed at once.
- **End sheet.** "The alarm road pulled you sideways." I knew it only as a nuisance, never a road.

## 2. The jump and landing: what it teaches vs. what it should

**Should teach (spec 3b):** stress leaves fewer places to land and a wider gap; the alarm road gains spines and pulls, as protection; supports and practice add spines back; the spark always arrives.

**Actually teaches:** "Steer onto a dot or rewind." The code checks angle and teleports; no leap.
- Unaimed, a 5-spine face catches about 67% of passes; legs 1-3 are near-automatic.
- Stress touches only leg 4 (22-32% coverage); attentive players still land first try.
- In-ride greens and reds never touch spines.
- The alarm road has no spines; its pull ends in an endless miss loop (C), against the spec's no-villain and always-arrives rules.

Takeaway: "stress makes aiming annoying."

**Redesign, "The Leap" (no added text):**
1. **Open the gap.** The tube ends in a dark cleft; the next face fills the screen for the last ~3 s, spines as big pads.
2. **Leap for real.** The spark arcs out with hang time; you steer in the air; a pad catches you with a click, and the next tube lights from that pad.
3. **Make stress physical.** Fewer thinking pads, wider cleft, longer hang.
4. **Give the alarm side spines.** Many bright, easy pads where the pull drifts you. Landing there is a real arrival: a short jittery red leg, the hand jerks away; the body protected you. Thinking pads are reachable only against the pull.
5. **Miss gently.** The spark scatters into the gap; a fresh spark fires from behind (neurons fire again). No rewind, no toast.
6. **Sprout in the open.** On leg 4, a green fills a dashed spine on the face ahead; a red dims one for a while.
7. **Remember the road.** "Ride the same week" keeps your last pad slightly bigger (session only): practice you can feel.

The player discovers that a hard week sends the signal down the alarm road and supports open the thinking road.

| # | Change | Why | Cost | Cut |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Build The Leap (section 2, steps 1, 2, 3 and 5) | Content becomes the mechanic; freedom to fail without punishment | L | Angle check, 9-unit rewind, "Missed the landing" toast |
| 2 | Alarm-road spines and an alarm arrival | Ends the loop; the pull becomes a choice; spec and trauma-informed floor | M | Pull with no destination |
| 3 | Leg-4 pickups sprout or dim spines on the face ahead | Supports visibly add places to land; experiment inside play | M | Replay captions that promise spine changes the player never sees |

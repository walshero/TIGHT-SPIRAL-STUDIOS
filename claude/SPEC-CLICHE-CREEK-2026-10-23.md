# SPEC: Cliché Creek (standalone) v0.1 Lab

Spec for `cliche-creek.html`. Lane: TSP repo (RW). Status: Lab.

## Founder words (primary source, 2026-10-23)

> "Create a standalone cliche creek game based on the mechanics of Kireji but with a new art take entirely. Paper craft to the max. Start the beta build with egregoous cliches and 3 solid formal rhyming poems. Each game has ONE poem mixed in and you sort the order of the lines once you id them all"

## Player

EN195 creative writing students at a community college, and any reader learning to hear a fresh line against a stock one. Phone first (iPhone), low vision a design floor (the founder has RP).

## Mechanics carried from Kireji (kireji-pond.html, Cliché Creek mode v5.6 to v5.13)

- A stream of single lines drifts past: one real poem broken into lines, mixed with cliché lines (ratio 1 real : 2 cliché).
- Tap a line, then judge it: Keep (real line), Tear up (cliché), Let it drift (comes round again).
- A real line comes at least every third boat. Lines that drift off come round again. Torn clichés leave for the round.
- Still water: the river stops; boats wait in place; a lever deals new boats. On by default under prefers-reduced-motion.
- Once every real line is kept, the sort: put the lines back in the poet's order.

## Changes from Kireji, on purpose

- Every judgment is answered at once (Kireji's creek gave no verdict at the catch). Tearing a real line does not succeed: it will not tear, counts a slip, and goes back in the creek. This closes the Kireji open item "Smash in the creek confirms a real line".
- Formal rhyming poems, not haiku. The sort offers a rhyme hint: each line tagged with its end sound, and the rhyme scheme shown above the slots.
- Lines placed correctly lock with a pin; the player fixes the rest.

## Poems (public domain in the US)

1. Robert Frost, "Dust of Snow" (New Hampshire, 1923). 8 lines, ABAB CDCD.
2. W. B. Yeats, "The Lake Isle of Innisfree" (1890). 12 lines, ABAB CDCD EFEF.
3. Gerard Manley Hopkins, "Pied Beauty" (Poems, ed. Bridges, 1918). Curtal sonnet, 11 lines, ABCABC DBCDC.

Order runs easy to hard. Verbatim text is a published-source claim: checked against the published text, see the FUNES row.

## Art

Paper craft, new to the studio: torn-paper layered hills with white deckle edges, pleated paper pines, a rosette sun, stitched paper clouds, an origami crane, paper-strip river, origami boats carrying line slips, a clothesline with pegs for caught lines, paper confetti when a cliché tears. Three modes in the corner: Day, Night, Ink (high contrast).

## Floors

Single file, offline, no external hosts, no emoji, scene first, 18px type, 44px touch, every text token computed against its surface. Machine copy is fallback for the founder to edit (HITL).

What this frame tests

  • Eye level, not overhead: you stand at the table instead of looking down at a floor plan.
  • Cut paper with real depth: every layer casts its own shadow, edges are hand-cut, the grain shows.
  • Light tells the hour: a low sun in the window, a lamp pooling warm on the wood.
  • A person without a face: the server's hand and sleeve set the plate; steam rises off the bread.

The cover's red stays as the wall, so the book's look carries over. If this lands, each regular gets the same treatment: Alan's stool, Trudy's red and sparkling water, Rick's coffee, Edie and Hal at the bar.

## 1. The pitch, in the studio's formula

It's like Tomy's Drive Yourself Crazy, but teaches how a nerve carries a message, and
has a dashboard that reads in meters per second.

## 2. Core loop (one route, 60 to 90 seconds)

1. STAKES. A body-sized event appears at the top of the window: a hand near a steaming
   kettle. No text. (Mamet)
2. PREDICT. One tap on one of two cards ("The fast lane gets there first" / "They
   arrive together"). The card stays pinned in the corner. (Coordinator)
3. RIDE. The belt scrolls toward the player across the full window. The spark steers
   freely, edge to edge. The speedometer shows conduction velocity.
4. LEAP. Bright gaps in the insulation (nodes) pass under the spark; each one fires a
   short hop and a woody click. On an insulated lane the hops come fast; on a bare lane
   the spark crawls. The player feels saltatory conduction before reading the word.
5. ARRIVE. The belt ends at the destination. The stakes resolve on stage: the hand
   lifts off the kettle.
6. NOTICE. The pinned prediction card flips: right or wrong, it shows what happened in
   one sentence. Then one refutation card. Then the temple bell (W6), once.
7. NAME. Only now does the vocabulary word appear on the toy's paper tape: "saltatory
   conduction: the signal jumps node to node." Tap to hear it read aloud.

## 3b. Outside and the changing nerve (reconvene 2026-11-05)

Life happens outside the window, and the nerve answers. A picture strip across the top
of the toy's window shows the outside; the full-width belt below shows the nerve. Each
picture changes the next gap the spark must jump.

The landing pads. At every gap between neurons (the confluence) the next neuron offers
landing pads: its dendritic spines. Calm baseline: five pads, close together. The spark
jumps the gap and lands on any pad.

| Outside picture | What happens to the pads | Time scale | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A test tomorrow (one hard moment) | Thinking-road pads DIM; two stay lit; they relight when the moment passes | Minutes | Arnsten 2009 |
| A week of short nights / phone at 2 a.m. | Thinking-road pads VANISH, five down to two; the gap widens | Days to weeks | Arnsten 2009; Radley 2006 |
| Any stressor | Alarm-road pads MULTIPLY and the belt drifts toward the alarm road | Minutes and longer | Arnsten 2009 |
| A night of sleep | Pads relight; consolidation | One night | Zagaar et al. 2013 (sleep and plasticity) |
| A walk or a run | New pads sprout (exercise raises BDNF, which supports new spines) | Days | van Praag review (PMC5880155) |
| A friend on the steps | Alarm road calms; thinking pads relight | Moments | SOURCE OWED (social buffering) |
| Riding the same road again | That road grows pads | Many rides | Use-dependent plasticity (LTP) |
| Time after the hard week ends | Vanished pads grow back | Weeks | Radley et al. 2005 |

STUDIO SIMPLIFICATION: pad counts and timing are a picture of connection strength,
compressed to play time. Most structural evidence is from rodents; the Library says so.

The two roads. The thinking road (prefrontal) and the alarm road (amygdala) run side by
side as bands of the belt. Stress strengthens the alarm road; that is protection, not
failure. Resilience in this game means balance and regrowth, never zero stress.

Trauma-informed floor (SAMHSA 2014, six principles):
- Everyday stressors only. No violence, abuse, injury, loss, emergency imagery, or jump scares.
- Every picture is listed in the Library before play; every nerve change has a "why" one tap away.
- The player can skip any picture, switch the outside layer off, or choose which stressors appear.
- Founder ruling 2026-11-05: three hits fades the signal (game over) with an instant return to the checkpoint. The alarm road is never the villain.
- Restorers include people. Restorers are what the world gives you, never homework.
- The game never asks or guesses how stressed the player is. No wellness claims.

## 3c. The Resilience Balance (MassBay toolkit + AFWI Resilience Scale)

The dashboard gains a second instrument beside the speedometer: a beam scale.
- Every outside picture drops a cube on the beam. Adversity cubes are heavy and
  rough-cut (red); support cubes are smooth and rounded (green); each carries a drawn
  object, so shape and object carry the meaning, never color alone.
- The beam's tilt sets the pad count at the next confluence: tipped to supports, five
  pads; level, four; tipped to adversity, two.
- The fulcrum is skills. Riding the same road again slides the fulcrum so the same cubes
  tip the beam less. This is the practice mechanic.
- Some adversity cubes arrive in pairs (missed bus, then late): stress stacks.
- Support cubes come from people and places as often as from the player: a bus that
  comes, a quiet room, a meal, a professor who answers, a friend.
- Every why-card opens "What's happening here." Never "what's wrong."
- The Library holds a "Where to go" card: 988 in the generic build; a dated, editable
  campus block (MassBay default from the toolkit's referral map) when switched on.
- The Bench becomes a balance loaded by hand: add cubes, take cubes away, practice to
  slide the fulcrum, then ride the result.
Credit line in the Library: the Resilience Balance as taught in the MassBay toolkit,
after the AFWI Resilience Scale. Our own drawing; no toolkit or AFWI art or text reproduced.

## 3d. Load a week (founder direction 2026-11-05, built in v0.1)

Before each ride the player loads a week onto the balance over the live tube. Ten cubes:
short night, test tomorrow, phone at 2 a.m., missed bus, loud room (stress, rough-cut);
real sleep, a walk, a friend, a good meal, practice (support, rounded). Preloaded with a
mixed week. Tilt sets the thinking-cortex spines (2 to 6) and the alarm pull; practice
moves the balance point. Each loaded cube appears as an outside picture during the
ride. Calm road skips the week. Nothing is saved.

## 3e. Bands, hits, checkpoints (founder ruling 2026-11-05, built in v0.2)

- Orange bands (frayed insulation) share one live width. Starting width comes from the
  week (more red cubes, wider).
- Bads widen every band and slow the spark: a red week card popping up, a red cube hit,
  a band hit. Greens narrow every band and never change speed (founder ruling, v0.4).
  Nothing speeds the spark up; nodes click and pulse at a steady cruise.
- Hits (red cubes, orange bands) fill three pips. Three hits: "The signal faded"; return
  to the last checkpoint with hits reset.
- Checkpoints: yellow double ring at each leg's halfway mark, and each relay landing.
- Every effect is named on screen with both consequences ("Bands wider. Slower.").
- Pace (v0.7): cruise 6.5 units/s at Normal; four speed stops (Gentle, Easy, Normal, Fast);
  each hit quietly trims cruise 10% (floor 72%), recovering while riding clean; bands every
  26-34 units, widest 32% of the tube; about 1.85 s warning at readable distance. After the
  Game Accessibility Guidelines (adjustable speed) and Xbox Accessibility Guideline 108.

## 4. Routes (the ride map)

MVP SLICE (build next session):
- R1 FIRST TOUCH. Fingertip to spinal cord. Teaches myelin, nodes, speed, one-way
  travel. Prediction: "Insulated band or bare band: which gets there first?"
  One outside beat: a short-nights picture takes the next confluence from five pads
  to two; a sleep picture brings them back before the end.
- THE BENCH. Two dials (insulation, axon width) and a strip of seven day-cards the
  player arranges (short night, test, walk, friend, practice); the belt runs the week
  they built. Free play, no goal. (Osterweil)
- YOUR OWN NERVE (if D5 approved). The window flashes; the player taps. The panel shows
  their measured time and lays a typical route over it: eye, visual cortex, motor
  cortex, spinal cord, thumb. Nothing stored, nothing compared, no "best."

FULL GAME (after playtest):
- R2 TWO PAINS. Bare foot on a brick. Two riders leave at once: a fast lane and a slow
  lane. The player rides both by switching. Teaches first pain and second pain.
- R3 THE FORK. Hand on the kettle. A fork at the spinal cord: the short road lifts the
  hand; the long road reaches the brain and the player "feels" it after the hand has
  moved. Teaches the reflex arc.
- R4 CROSSING. The belt ends at a gap. The spark becomes a cloud of messengers; the
  player steers them into receptors by shape (not color). Some receptors excite, some
  calm. Teaches synapse, excitation, inhibition.
- R5 UP THE LADDER. Spinal cord to thalamus to the sensory strip. The destination is a
  map of the body on the cortex (OpenStax homunculus figure, CC BY). Teaches that the
  brain keeps a body map.
- R6 THE LONG WEEK. Seven days of outside pictures, chosen or default. The player
  sees pads thin, sees the alarm road pull, and finds the restorers. Ends a week later
  with the pads grown back. Teaches stress, plasticity, and recovery.
- Spaced return: R2-R5 each reuse one earlier decision (the player must pick the
  insulated lane to beat the clock), so retrieval happens inside play, not in a quiz.

## 5. Learning design, with the evidence

- Content is the mechanic (Habgood & Ainsworth 2011: more learning, and seven times the
  free-choice play time, for the intrinsic version).
- Predict, then ride, then refute (Schroeder & Kucera 2022: refutation text g = 0.41
  across 44 comparisons). Refutation cards target real misconceptions only:
  1. "A nerve signal is electricity racing down a wire." It is regenerated node by node and peaks near 120 m/s.
  2. "You feel pain the instant you touch the stove." The spinal reflex moves the hand first.
  3. "Reflexes come from the brain." The arc closes in the spinal cord.
  4. "Signals can travel both ways down an axon." The refractory membrane behind the signal blocks the way back.
  5. "Stress breaks your brain for good." Connections thinned by a long hard stretch grew back in animal studies once the stress eased.
  6. "Stress makes your whole brain weaker." The alarm road gets stronger; it is built to protect you.
  7. "Your brain is set once you grow up." Connections keep changing with use throughout life.
- Vocabulary after experience, never before (Gee, just-in-time; 2012 sabbatical report).
- No extrinsic loop: the trip odometer reads meters of nerve and milliseconds, which are
  content, not points (studio redline; Nicholson critique in the 2012 report).
- No brain-training claim anywhere (Coordinator veto).

## 6. Art direction: Brainbow Lab, ridden like S.T.U.N. Runner (D12, ruled 2026-11-05)

- Look: multicolor neurons glowing on black, in the style of Brainbow fluorescence
  imaging (Livet et al., 2007). Studio drawings, no lab images reproduced.
- Camera: behind the rider, inside the axon tube, after Atari's S.T.U.N. Runner (1989).
  Left/right steers around the full circumference, walls included. The tube turns with
  the player by default; "Tube stays still" is a comfort option, and reduced-motion
  devices start with it.
- Tube: cyan rings with eight colored lengthwise fibers; bright white rings are nodes
  (click and camera pulse, no speed change, v0.4); frayed bands are amber slabs with dark
  diagonal stripes and loose edge fibers (slow you on contact, v0.4); speed streaks run
  along the walls (off under reduced motion).
- Backdrop (v0.6): Cajal's drawings of each region, dimmed to roots, with lightning that
  lights them only along each bolt. In front, the circuit we ride: nine fibers beside our
  axon joined by cell bodies and cross-links, signals racing along and jumping links. Two
  thorny alarm fibers fire harder after a bad while thinking fibers dim (Arnsten 2009);
  after a green, thinking fibers brighten and cell bodies grow new tips (Radley 2005;
  BDNF). No floating, unconnected cells.
- Each relay ends in a gap and a magenta dendrite face with lit spines; missing spines
  show as dashed outlines. Under a stress-tipped week, a magenta glow marks the alarm
  road's pull.
- HUD: balance gauge (bottom left), leg name with meters and milliseconds (center),
  speed in meters per second (bottom right), outside picture inset (top right).

## 7. Sound

Motor hum whose pitch tracks speed (the toy's whir, softened). Woody click on each node
leap. A spatialized cue (left, center, right) half a second before each obstacle. Temple
bell (W6) once per finished route. Silence option in the comfort corner.

## 8. Accessibility (design constraints, not a feature list)

- Full field (founder ruling 2026-11-05): the belt fills the whole viewing window, edge
  to edge, and steering is free across it. No center-column restriction, no narrowed belt.
- Sound adds a channel: every obstacle has a positioned cue before it arrives.
- Inputs: drag, hold-left/hold-right, arrow keys; two-switch scanning and tilt as options.
- Comfort corner (live, never a wall): speed (four stops, the slowest steps one tile per
  tap), sound, Paper / Dim / Night mode button.
- Type: 20px body, 18px floor, near-black on cream; controls 44px minimum.
- Shapes and textures carry meaning, never color alone. Reduced-motion steps the belt.

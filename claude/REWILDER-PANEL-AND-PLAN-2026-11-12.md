# Rewilder: design panel review and forward plan

Date: 2026-11-12. Build reviewed: v0.26. Build that followed: v0.27.
Home: `lab/rewilder.html`, branch `rewilder-v0.1`, PR #55.

## What this is, and what it is not

The founder asked for the studio's game design voices to study the game and report what works,
what does not, and what to do next. He asked for six months of play. That did not happen. What
happened: a script walked levels 1 to 4 and read out their sizes, and the panel argued from those
facts and from the week's build history.

The voices are seats modeled on what each person has published. None of these people took part:
Scot Osterweil, Eric Zimmerman, Peter Stidwill, James Paul Gee, Sherry Turkle, Jane McGonigal.

## The panel's read

| Seat | Working | Not working |
|---|---|---|
| Osterweil (four freedoms) | Level 1 is the game. Each building falls for its own reason, and failing costs a floor, not the run. | Level 3 is level 1 with the dial turned up. The fortress is a different game bolted on. |
| Zimmerman (meaningful play) | Smashing has clear, immediate results. | The only decision is one card per site, and both cards win. The dam's two cards were a false choice. |
| Stidwill (learning game production) | The game shows before it tells in level 1. | Fifty hints is a tutorial. Most explain what the player would find by trying. |
| Gee (probing, learning by doing) | Weight, balance and legs teach by probing. This is the strongest asset. | Nothing asks the player to predict before the system answers. |
| Turkle (what the simulation teaches) | The salmon's death is the one moment with weight, and it is played straight. | Energy never ran out, so nothing was ever at stake for the animals. |
| McGonigal (why players return) | Clear goal, clear feedback. | No record, no reason to come back, and the end card led with quitting. |

## Forward plan (filed, not built)

### Level 3: the dome

Founder, as transcribed: "I think that you should build up in like an ecotarium where people can live.
It's like farming and ... agricultural residents in like a big Epcot Center ball kind of thing."
"when you leave, that gets taken over and turned into like think of like the sphere and it has like the
Eye of Sauron and it has like other like funny evil evil things."
On whether the player smashes the sphere: the founder agreed that the player smashes the corruption on
it, not the dome itself. "It also has to be fun gameplay."

Shape:

1. Level 2 stays as reclaim, then two empty lots.
2. In level 3 the lots become one dome: stacked farms and homes inside a sphere. It gains a floor as the
   player feeds it.
3. On the next vacation the Baron takes it: an eye, billboards, a cage, antennas.
4. The player climbs the sphere's ribs and knocks each piece of his gear loose. It tumbles down the
   curve. The dome underneath survives and the people come back out.
5. This replaces the fortress.

Open questions for a convening before any build:

- What does the player do to grow the dome? Hauled river junk is one candidate.
- How does climbing a curve work with the game's grid of square blocks?
- What do the people inside do that the player can see from the street?
- Sources: any real project the dome stands for needs research and a citation, or a studio-invention label.

### Level 4: the island

Founder, as transcribed: "level four. There's a bridge to go over the ocean to an island, and the island
is where the billionaires used to be."

Shape: a bridge off the left edge, over the salmon's sea, to an island with new sites. The world gets
wider, which the game needs anyway: four empty lots is all the current street holds.

Open: what stands on the island, and what the player grows there.

### The wetland

Removed as a dam card in v0.27. Candidate: the beaver builds it upstream from hauled junk, as its own
act in its own place. The real-world fact behind it (a leaky stick dam holds storm water) stays in the
field notes.

### Carried from the panel, not yet ruled

- Gee: ask for a prediction before the system answers. The Lens is the natural place.
- Zimmerman: let the order of growing change what is possible next.
- McGonigal: a per-level record on the end card beyond the title-screen best.

## Update, same evening: the founder replaced the dome plan (built in v0.30)

Founder, exact words: "Building the dinky little lots into things feels like a chore not a reward after we’ve already built the first 5. Each level should expand the world. The fortress tower gets rebuild as an eco-residence. A green tower. the data center gets built back as an art center. Both are thoughtful playful beautiful spaces. The arts center is circular like the sphere in Las Vegas, and when it gets taken over in level 3 it has the eye or Sauron on it (changes periodically). This time when you restore level 3, the three characters chase the baron back to his island hideout, which is now swimmable at left"

| Earlier plan in this file | What v0.30 built instead |
|---|---|
| Two empty lots after reclaiming | Removed. |
| Level 3 dome on the lots, corrupted by the Baron | The data center regrows as a round arts center in level 2. In level 3 the Baron covers it in screens (an eye, a dollar sign, OBEY). Smashing the screens brings it back. |
| The fortress replaced by the dome | The fortress stays as the level 3 climb and regrows as a green tower of homes. |
| Level 4 island by bridge | The island opens when level 3 is grown. The animals swim to it and bring down the hideout. No bridge. |

Still open: what level 4 and later should add once the island has been won, and whether the wetland returns.

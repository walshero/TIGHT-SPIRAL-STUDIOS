Playing what you know mints coins. Coins buy free play. The Semester Machine always pays.

The Workshop Vending Machine Studio exception. Live backend, not offline.

Four cabinets, one per workshop. Feed the machine your draft: certify it, paste the link, paste the words, and it mints a coin and puts your name on the board. This is the one corner of the arcade that talks to a server, because a shared class board can't live in a single offline file. Everything else in this arcade stays exactly as it was: single file, nothing stored.

The Semester Machine

Sandbag Drop

Every extra word is a sandbag. Tap a word to cut it. Tap again to put it back. Cut enough and the balloon rises.

Line Break

Here is a famous sentence with its breaks removed. Tap a slash to break the line there. Every break is valid. The game names what your break does.

Text: William Carlos Williams, "The Red Wheelbarrow" (1923, public domain).

How the studio works

How this was made

Built by Tight Spiral Studios with Claude in the loop. The coin economy is a credited lesson from Lure of the Labyrinth (Learning Games Network / MIT Education Arcade, Scot Osterweil): learning embedded in play, earn in one room and spend in another, mystery-first entry. All art is hand-cut SVG made in-file; the coin sound is synthesized in-file with Web Audio; the Line Break text is William Carlos Williams, 1923, public domain, attributed. Everything above the Workshop Vending Machine stores nothing and runs in memory only. The coin ticket is the player's to carry out. The Workshop Vending Machine is the one exception: what you feed it (your certification, group, link, full text, and email) is sent to a server so your workshop can see who has submitted.

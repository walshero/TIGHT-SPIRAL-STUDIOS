## 1. The finding that reorders the build: Georgia is not a modern-mechanic scene

*Seats: Fact-Check Lead (HALT), Even-handedness bench, Coherence Skeptic, Todd Gitlin (guest), Continuity/Lore Keeper.*

`cyl-v6-rebuild-spec.md` §3 splits the game's second frame by era. Cold War scenes turn a **declassified record**. Modern scenes show **two sourced contemporary readings side by side, and the game refuses to adjudicate** — because for a 2020s president "there is no NARA reveal; there is contested interpretation."

Founder ruling 6 collides with that directly, and ruling 6 wins, because the premise under §3 is false for this scene.

**For Georgia, January 2, 2021, a record exists, and it is unusually complete:**

- 62 lawsuits across nine states and DC, nearly all dismissed, 30 on the merits, some by Trump-appointed judges.
- Attorney General Barr, December 2020: no fraud on a scale that could have affected the outcome.
- CISA Director Krebs fired in November, after saying there was no indication of widespread fraud.
- The call itself: one hour, January 2, 2021, to Georgia's Republican Secretary of State. *"I just want to find 11,780 votes, which is one more than we have, because we won the state."* Same call: the ballots are corrupt; it is more illegal for you than it is for them.

That is not contested interpretation. That is courts, the sitting administration's own Attorney General, its own cybersecurity director, and a recording of the principal. **Georgia runs the Cold War mechanic: the record turns.** It is the most documented scene in the build, not the least.

**Gitlin (posthumous doppelganger; d. 2022, drawn from the published work, seated here as frame analysis and labeled as such so no later session mistakes this for a consultation):** the side-by-side is a framing device with a politics of its own. Two panels of equal width is an argument that the world has two halves of equal weight. Where the record is asymmetric, symmetry is the distortion. *The Whole World Is Watching* was about exactly this: the balance ritual as a producer of false pictures.

**Even-handedness bench, amending its own standard:** even-handedness governs **selection discipline** — real, dated, attributed, chosen for ethos rather than for convenience — and it does **not** require symmetric presentation. The bench had been reading its own constitution as a parity rule. It is a sourcing rule. Recorded as an amendment, because the misreading was about to ship.

**Where the §3 two-readings mechanic survives:** it is correct for a genuinely unsettled question and should be held for a future scene that has one. It is wrong here. Do not delete it; park it.

**Fact-Check Lead, HALT cleared with two conditions.** Every claim in §5's fog layer carries date, speaker and source before the scene ships. And the Ramsland 23-page Michigan report — denounced by Michigan's own election officials — enters as **fog material, never as a competing reading**. It has the shape of evidence: a page count, forensic vocabulary, a named author. That shape is the instrument. It is what a real citizen had to sort in real time.

## 2. Question one: replace the survey mechanic

*Seats: Measurement Design bench (HALT), AI-Skeptic (HALT), Variable-Reward Honesty (HALT), Game-Feel Designer, Affordance Director, Learning Scientist, Filament Games (guest), Jared Horvath (guest), Sherry Turkle (guest), Trauma-Informed (HALT), Peak-End.*

**The founder's charge:** rating on a scale is measurement dressed as play, and it is leading — it tells the player the answer is trust and that a correction is coming. Replace it with a commitment that costs something.

The bench splits the charge in two, because the two halves have different fixes.

**Filament Games:** the learning objective has to be the verb of the game. The stated verb is *decide under fog*. The built verb is *rate*. Those are not cousins. A player who rates is a respondent; a player who decides is a participant. Everything downstream inherits whichever verb the beat actually uses, and right now the whole instrument inherits *respondent*.

**AI-Skeptic, HALT:** the five-point capture is a survey with a period typeface on it. It is the form-shaped default any system reaches for when it wants a number. It ships because it is measurable, not because it is true.

**Horvath (guest):** the leading problem is structural, and it is worse than the wording. A pre-test/post-test frame teaches the player what the experiment is about by its shape alone. By the second ask they are not answering the question; they are answering *the design*. And whatever they learn, they will encode the correction — the thing that moved — rather than the noticing. You will have built a machine that teaches "I was fooled," which is a fact about them, not a skill.

**The bench's diagnosis of why it read as leading, which nobody had named:** the record always corrects. In a build where every record turn reverses the player, the pre/post structure is a tell, and a fair player reads it by the second scene. **The fix is not only in the mechanic — it is that the record must sometimes confirm.** In the Kennedy scene, a player who commits to caution and flags Moscow's reading as decisive should meet a record that says: that is what the room did, and it held. Vindication has to be genuinely available or the instrument is a trap wearing a stopwatch.

### The replacement: THE STANDING ORDER

Same grammar as The Carry. You spend something scarce, it persists visibly, and the next beat is materially different because of it.

**You do not rate the claim. You act on it, and the act stays on screen.**

| Beat | What the player does | What is measured |
|---|---|---|
| 0 — The room | The Carry: six objects, three slots (PROP 002, unchanged) | What you could see when you committed |
| 1 — The fog | Flag which unknown is decisive | Problem-solving read |
| 2 — **The order** | **Issue exactly one order from era-plausible acts. It is stamped, it goes out, and it stays on screen for the rest of the scene.** | The posture — leadership read |
| 3 — The fog thickens | New claim arrives wearing the shape of evidence (Ramsland; for JFK, a contradicting advisory read) | Media-literacy read |
| 3.5 — The record turns | The audio plays | — |
| 4 — **Stand or countermand** | **Your order is still standing. You may let it stand, or countermand it — and countermanding is visibly costly, because the first order already went out.** | The delta, behavioral |
| 5 — The both-and | Consequence (sourced) + mirror (no-fault) | — |

**Why this is not a survey.** There is no scale, so there is no implied direction and no midpoint to hide in. The commitment is an act with a stated cost, disclosed before it is paid. The delta the game records is categorical and behavioral — *stood / countermanded / countermanded after the audio* — not a movement along an attitude line.

**Why it stops leading.** At the order beat there is no pre/post shape to read, because the player does not yet know a second ask exists. And standing pat is a fully respectable outcome that the mirror reads warmly. A player who issued a cautious order and let it stand is not a player who failed to learn.

**Turkle (guest), on the audio and the order together:** the recording does something a transcript cannot. A transcript is a claim about what was said; a voice is the thing itself, with its pauses and its pressure, and it arrives in the body before it arrives in the argument. Put it *after* the player's own order is already on screen and you have built the rarest thing in a civic game — a moment where the player is holding their own commitment in one hand and the record in the other, and nobody is telling them what to feel about either. That is the peak. Do not narrate it.

**Peak-End:** agreed, and that is the peak of the whole build. Protect it. No caption over the audio, no counter, no prompt until it finishes.

**Variable-Reward Honesty, HALT cleared:** the cost is real, disclosed and reversible-with-consequence rather than punitive. It teaches anticipation instead of manufacturing compulsion. Clean.

**Trauma-Informed, HALT with one condition:** the countermand must never be worded as a confession. Not *"changed your mind?"* Not *"in light of the record."* The wording is closer to: **your order is standing. Anything to add?** The player is still the decider at Beat 4, not a defendant. The mirror's three cases from the Carry bench extend cleanly: ordered and stood / ordered and countermanded / countermanded only after the voice. None is scored.

**Measurement Design bench, HALT cleared with the claim ceiling narrowed:** this strengthens Brief A's predicted answer rather than overturning it. One construct — *judgment under incomplete information* — with facets, now read off behavior rather than self-report, which is a straight upgrade in construct validity. But the ipsative pre/post number that the v5 AAC&U crosswalk leaned on is **gone**, and the crosswalk's headline claim goes with it. That is handled in §3.

**PROP 003 — THE STANDING ORDER (props room deposit, content-independent):**

```
ORDERS[]  : { id, text, era-plausible, consequence: sourced, none flagged correct }
COMMIT    : exactly one, stamped, visible for the remainder of the scene
DISCLOSURE: the cost is stated before the order is issued, never after
TURN      : record opens AFTER the order is on screen, never before
RECOMMIT  : { stand | countermand -> orderId }, wording carries no verdict
DELTA     : categorical { stood, countermanded, countermanded-post-audio }
FLOORS    : travels with the part - 46px targets, list parity, keyboard,
            focus, reduced motion, aria-live on stamp and countermand,
            no colour-as-sole-signal, transcript parity for every audio event
```

Reusable wherever a scene must make a judgment cost something: a grade appeal, a triage bay, a disciplinary hearing, an editorial call.

### Recommendation

1. **Recommended.** Ship The Standing Order as Beats 2 and 4 of both scenes, paired with The Carry at Beat 0. Deposit PROP 003.
2. **Why.** It fixes both halves of the founder's charge with one move — the verb becomes *decide*, and the leading structure dissolves because there is no scale and no visible pre/post shape. It reuses the grammar the studio already proved at Beat 0, so the player learns one interaction model and the bench iterates against one pattern. And it produces the peak the build has never had: your own order, on screen, when the voice comes in.
3. **Tradeoffs.** The clean ipsative number is gone, and with it the sharpest line in the AAC&U crosswalk. Authoring rises: every order needs a sourced consequence, not just a flavor line. Beat 2 gets heavier, and the two scenes now have to carry a build that used to spread its weight over six.
4. **Simpler alternative.** Binary standing order — one order, then stand-or-countermand with no menu. Two screens, a quarter of the authoring, passes all five Fingers binaries. This is the fallback if the sourced-consequence load bites during the Georgia build.
5. **More advanced.** Persist the order across both scenes: what you ordered in 1962 is still standing when the 2021 room opens, and the river at Beat 5 draws a posture across sixty years rather than two nights. Do not build until the single-scene loop is founder-locked. The PM seat holds this one.

**What to ignore.** Confidence sliders attached to the order — that is the survey climbing back in through the window. A timer on the order beat — punishes reading speed, hostile to low vision, and scarcity is already in the slots. Scoring the order. Any "are you sure?" dialog, which converts cost into nagging.

## 3. Question two: the leadership rating instrument

*Seats: Assessment & Outcomes Specialist, Measurement Design bench (HALT), Skeptical Faculty Adopter, Fact-Check Lead, Learning Scientist.*

**Founder's correction, verified and upheld:** there is no AAC&U leadership VALUE rubric. The set is 16, and leadership is not among them. Verified against AAC&U directly — the 16 grew out of the Essential Learning Outcomes and include Critical Thinking, Problem Solving, Inquiry and Analysis, Civic Engagement, Ethical Reasoning, Information Literacy, Integrative Learning, Reading, Written and Oral Communication, Quantitative Literacy, Teamwork, Creative Thinking, Intercultural Knowledge and Competence, Global Learning, and Foundations and Skills for Lifelong Learning. No Leadership.

**The bench's answer: the absence is not a gap to fill. It is the finding.**

Leadership is not absent from the 16 because AAC&U overlooked it. It is absent because it is not a construct at that grain — it decomposes, under assessment, into judgment, ethical reasoning, problem-solving and civic capacity, which are all in the set. Shopping for a leadership rubric means importing an instrument built for a different purpose and wearing it as a credential.

**What the alternatives actually are, and what to ignore:**

- **Kouzes & Posner Student LPI** — 30-item self-report across five practices. Self-report is precisely what §2 just removed. **Ignore.**
- **Social Change Model / MSL (Astin's Seven C's)** — the real higher-ed standard, and a genuinely good *vocabulary* for civic leadership. But the instrument is again self-report, and the model is built for co-curricular development programs over a semester, not a six-minute decision. **Borrow the vocabulary if a line needs it; do not adopt the instrument.**
- **CAS leadership program standards** — program-level, not learner-level. Wrong altitude. **Ignore.**
- **Situational judgment tests** — a scenario, a forced choice among plausible responses, scored against behavioral keys. Decades of validity evidence as a selection and assessment method. **This is the match.**

**The finding, plainly: The Standing Order makes CYL a situational judgment test.** That is not a metaphor. Scenario, era-plausible response set, forced single commitment, behavioral scoring. The build has been reaching for a rubric when it had already become an instrument family with its own literature.

**Two amendments the trauma rails require**, which make this an honest SJT rather than a borrowed one:

1. **Descriptive, not normative scoring.** Classical SJTs score against expert consensus — a right answer. CYL cannot, and does not want to. It reports the player's **posture profile** — what you ordered, what you could see when you ordered it, whether you named the decisive unknown, whether you stood. No key, no percentile, no comparison to other players.
2. **No claim of predictive validity.** An SJT used for selection claims it forecasts job performance. CYL claims nothing of the kind and must say so in the instructor panel.

**The honest-claim ceiling, drafted for the founder's HITL** (Brief A's one required sentence, now writable):

> *Choose Your Leader is a situational judgment exercise. It records what you decided, what you could see when you decided it, and whether you changed your mind when the record opened. It reports those as a description of your reasoning, not as a score, and it does not claim to measure or predict leadership ability.*

**Where the institutional evidence actually lands, when it is time (§4):** the anchor is **Problem Solving** — in the 16, and a direct read on Beats 1, 2 and 4. **Critical Thinking** holds its v5 claim, and its "influence of context and assumptions" dimension is arguably *better* served by a behavioral commitment than by the old rating delta. **Civic Engagement** (knowledge and identity dimensions only, never participation). **Information Literacy** and **Ethical Reasoning** ride the fog layer. Leadership comes off the claim list entirely and stays where it belongs: the **subject** of the game, not its score.

**Assessment & Outcomes Specialist, on the cost:** the v5 crosswalk's headline — the ipsative delta as direct rubric evidence for the least-assessable Critical Thinking dimension — was a genuinely strong claim and it dies with the survey. The replacement claim is weaker on paper and stronger in fact: a categorical behavioral record beats a self-reported attitude shift, but it will not produce the tidy number that reads well in a Provost's deck. The founder should know that trade is being made deliberately.

**Skeptical Faculty Adopter:** good. A number I do not trust is worse than a description I can read. And "it does not claim to predict leadership ability" is the sentence that gets this past a curriculum committee rather than into a fight with one.

## 4. Question three: map outcomes last

*Seats: PM (SCOPE HALT), Assessment & Outcomes Specialist, Archivist, Coherence Skeptic, FERPA/Governance Steward.*

The bench affirms the founder's sequencing and adds the tripwire that makes it hold, because "map it later" without a mechanism becomes "map it never" or, worse, "design toward it quietly."

**The rule, proposed for locking:**

1. **No outcome language in the build files.** Not in copy, not in comments, not in an instructor panel, until both scenes are founder-locked. An outcome named in the source is an outcome the design starts serving.
2. **The crosswalk is a separate document, written after the lock**, against what the build *emits* — not a target the build is steered toward. This is the same discipline as sourcing a claim after the scene exists rather than writing the scene to fit a claim.

## 5. What rulings 2, 3 and 4 do to the room

*Seats: Compositor (HALT), Fact-Check Lead (HALT), ADA (HALT), Affordance & Permissions Steward (HALT), Screen Optimization Critic, Engagement Specialist.*

The Carry lattice survives. Rulings 2–4 change what two of its six objects *do*.

**The sequence (ruling 3), which also resolves an open call:** the television is not a competing aperture to the paper; it is the **first** one. The Carry bench left open how to seat the player in a domestic room without a caption saying so. The answer is now in the sequence: **the newscast plays, and then the paper opens.** The room tells you what the country is being told, and then you look at what the country was told *this morning* — and it is already wrong. No caption states the decision is in the room. The order of the two media objects states it.

**The paper (ruling 4) — headlines that inform the scene, and the deeper move.** The Carry bench's line for the paper was *"dated this morning; it was already out of date when it was printed."* Ruling 4 is not in tension with that — it completes it. The headlines are real, dated, attributed, and they genuinely inform: Berlin, the midterm campaign, the Cuba rumors that were circulating without the photographs. **The paper is accurate and blind at the same time.** That is the media-literacy instrument working without a word of instruction, and it is the same move the Georgia paper makes with the claims circulating on January 2.

**Audio — two rights positions, and they are not the same.** The Fact-Check Lead flags a distinction the handoff's phrasing collapses.

- **Kennedy.** The **address** of October 22, 1962 is a work of the United States government: public domain, available from the JFK Library and NARA, clean to ship. A **newscast** of that address is network archive material with rights attached and is not clean. **Recommendation: use the address.** It is also the truer artifact — it is what was actually in that room that night. If the newscast texture is wanted, build it from the PD address with studio-written framing that is visibly labeled as studio-written. No impersonation, no synthesized voice of a real person — the v5 rule holds and does not bend for period flavor.
- **Georgia.** The call recording — reported by the Washington Post, obtained by Georgia Public Broadcasting — is genuinely unsettled as to clearance, and there is a real argument it is a Georgia public record. **That needs a rights opinion, not a bench guess.** Opened as Brief F below.

**The build move that honors ruling 2 without waiting on the lawyers:** build the **audio slot**, per the v5 named-swappable-asset pattern, with the **transcript on screen as the floor**. The transcript is required by the ADA seat regardless — captions are not optional — so the accessible build and the clearance-safe build are the same build. The clip drops into the slot the day clearance lands. The scene is playable and honest before that day, because the quoted words are established fact, quotable and widely reproduced.

**ADA, HALT conditions on audio, non-negotiable:** off by default, never autoplay, keyboard-reachable, every sound paired with a visible event, full transcript parity, and no information carried by audio alone. All inherited from the v5 sound rails; none released by ruling 5.

**Affordance & Permissions Steward:** audio is a permission-gated capability. The build must work fully when it is denied, and must not nag. A player who never turns the sound on still gets the whole scene.

**Continuity catch — canon contradicts itself on the day count.** `cyl-v6-rebuild-spec.md` §8 says the address was "day eight." `cyl-v6-carry-and-fingers-bench.md` says "day six." The record: the U-2 photographed the sites October 14; Kennedy was briefed the morning of October 16; he addressed the country the evening of October 22. **Six days of weighing, from the briefing to the broadcast.** Both existing numbers are defensible depending on where you start counting, which is exactly why the build must say one thing. **Recommendation: "six days," counted from the briefing** — it matches the Carry bench, it is the count the man in the chair would have used, and it retires the ambiguous "day eight" phrasing.

## 9. Addendum — Hitchcock and Gitlin, summoned after the close

*Founder asked for both. Gitlin was already seated (§1); Hitchcock is a summons — not a standing seat, brought in on the suspense-versus-surprise question. Both are doppelgangers from the published work; Gitlin's is posthumous, as noted above.*

**Gitlin** (his standing caucus rule in the OS: *name the frame and what it excludes; an invisible frame is the real failure mode, and fluency is its symptom*):

The bench killed the both-sides scoreboard and built its mirror image without noticing. An order menu is a frame. A Georgia menu with no order that takes the caller seriously has decided the question before the player sits down, and the fluency of The Standing Order hides that. The fix is not a fraud-is-real option; the record forbids it. The fix is the Glass Engine move: **the frame names its own exclusion on screen.** *Every order on this desk assumes the count is sound. Here is why the office could assume that in January 2021.* Then the sources. An informed citizen is not someone who got the right answer; it is someone who can see the frame they were handed.

On the newspaper: the paper's blindness is only an instrument if the player can later see *what it excluded and why*. The missiles were not in the morning edition because the government had not released them, not because the press failed. Different exclusion, different lesson. Name which one.

**Hitchcock** (suspense versus surprise; the bomb under the table):

Surprise is fifteen seconds. Suspense is fifteen minutes. The record turn is a surprise mechanic — the player does not know, the bomb goes off, they flinch, it is over — and that is the structural reason the pre/post read as a trap. Surprise *is* a trap; the audience resents it the second time.

The two scenes sit on opposite sides of that line, and the bench flattened them. A nineteen-year-old in the Kennedy room genuinely does not know how it ends: surprise. The same player in the Georgia room already knows the outcome: suspense, the bomb visible to the audience while the room chats. Georgia does not need the record to reveal anything. It needs the player to sit with their own order on screen, knowing what is coming down the line, and the game doing nothing to hurry it. That is the peak Turkle described, and it is the second reason Georgia builds first: the mechanic is naturally suspense-shaped there.

Kennedy is therefore the harder scene, not the reference scene. Moving it from surprise toward suspense means giving the player something the room does not have — which is what the media prelude (Beat −1) was always for. Keep it, and make it do more work.

**Where they collide, and the ruling.** Gitlin wants the exclusion named before the order. Hitchcock says naming it defuses the bomb. The resolution is sequence, not compromise: **the frame-exclusion line lands at Beat 3 — after the order is stamped, before the audio.** The player commits inside the frame, then sees the frame, then hears the voice. Suspense preserved, frame visible, mandate served.

**Consequences for the dispatch table:**

- **Brief G amended.** The Georgia fog layer now carries the frame-exclusion line and its sources as a required element, and the order menu must include the caller's ask as an order with a sourced consequence — so the frame excludes nothing silently.
- **Brief B amended** the same way for 1962: the paper's exclusion (withheld, not missed) is named at Beat 3.
- **Build order confirmed:** Georgia first, for a second reason now on record.

## 10. Addendum — Horvath on the core mechanic (2026-09-16), and the founder's pick

*Founder asked what Horvath says about the core mechanic of the scene. He was summoned back alone. Then the founder chose option 5 below, which overrides the "do not build until the single-scene loop is founder-locked" caveat; that override is a founder call and is recorded as one.*

**Horvath:** the core is not the order, it is *predict, observe, update* — the only sequence the brain treats as learning. An order is a prediction about yourself; the record cannot contradict it. A prediction about the *world* — what the order will meet — is what the record can land on, and a prediction error is what memory is built from. So the stamp carries a forecast: one tap, three era-plausible outcomes, none flagged. The record turn stops being exposure and becomes an error signal; stand-or-countermand becomes the update.

Confidence is not optional — hypercorrection (errors made with high confidence are the ones people fix) is the strongest finding in the field — but it does not need a slider. It is already diegetic in the order sets: a public order is high-confidence, a quiet one is hedged. Read it off how loud the order is, never ask.

Load: three selection tasks before the peak is more than working memory holds in six minutes. The fog flag adds a choice without adding information, because what you carried already fixes what you can read. Fold it; the forecast is the problem-solving read; Beats 1 and 2 become one screen, which is the founder's own one-thing rule honored instead of nodded at.

Retrieval: memory is what you pull back out. Before the river, one tap — what did you order in 1962? — and then the river. A wrong answer is the most honest line in the game.

On the two open calls: Kennedy must be able to confirm (confirmed predictions strengthen too), but the biggest effect is the loud order that meets a contradicting record, so both paths must be real. In Georgia the player already knows the count stands, so the forecast is about *cost*: what did saying no meet? Threats to the office and the family, the December 1 warning borne out, the 2023 indictment. Hitchcock keeps his suspense; the prediction error survives.

What he cannot have: the delayed test. The privacy floor forbids remembering the player. The instructor panel says so; the instructor is the spacing.

**Recommendation as put to the founder:** (1) forecast at the stamp, fog folded, confidence diegetic, recall tap, Georgia forecasts cost; (2) turns the record turn into a prediction error and cuts a beat; (3) Brief E grows — every order needs a sourced outcome; Brief A gets a paragraph, because the forecast replaces the flag as the problem-solving read; (4) simpler: recall tap only; (5) advanced: the forecast-confidence read across both scenes — a player loud and contradicted twice gets a different mirror than one who hedged twice.

**Founder chose 5.** Built 2026-09-16 as two-scene alpha v0.2: forecast on the stamp (public/quiet marked), fog read-only on the order screen, per-scene mirror on hit/miss × loud/quiet, a sourced cost line in every Georgia consequence, the recall tap, and a cross-scene read above the river. The honest-claim ceiling gains four words: *what you expected it to meet*.

**Brief E amended:** sourced outcome per order, both scenes, plus the Georgia cost line (Reuters June 2021; *Integrity Counts*, 2021; Georgia SoS, Dec 1, 2020; Fulton County indictment, Aug 14, 2023) — all Alpha fidelity until the Fact-Check Lead runs them. **Brief A amended:** the problem-solving facet now reads off the forecast, not a flag.

## 11. The rethink — the player is the citizen (founder ruling, 2026-09-16)

**Founder:** "Why would I be carrying the coffee table or the picture into the next level. This is poor game design. What choices REALLY matter here? We need a rethink for this game to land."

**The diagnosis, accepted.** The Carry was attention-allocation wearing an inventory costume. It half-worked when the objects were a telephone and a newspaper and collapsed the moment the release plate's real furniture went in. Nobody carries a coffee table into a decision. The bench fixed the picture (Reference Wrangler) and nobody re-asked whether the verb survived. Grievance recorded.

**The mismatch underneath it.** The mandate says build *for the informed citizen*. The v6 spine seated the player *in the leader's chair*. They pulled against each other all week. The plate settles it: a living room, a swivel chair with its back to you, facing the set. A citizen's chair.

**Ruling: the player is the citizen, not the leader.** This reverses the v6 spine for these two scenes. The two nights become a matched pair:
- **October 22, 1962** — a leader asks for your trust on evidence you cannot see (no photographs are shown in the address; they go to the UN three days later). The record vindicates the trust.
- **January 2–3, 2021** — a leader asks for your distrust on evidence you can see but cannot evaluate (a 23-page report, an arena video), after the record has already checked it. The record refutes the distrust.

Mirror images. Not both-sides: the pair teaches a *method* — how you decide whom to believe when you cannot check yourself. The retired v5 line stays retired; this is not rating trust, it is acting on a claim in front of someone who will remember.

**The choices that really matter, for a citizen in that chair:**
1. **Who do you turn to.** One aperture, not three carries. The set, the hi-fi on the shortwave band (Radio Moscow — genuinely what listeners heard), the telephone, the paper. In 2021: cable, the group chat (which is where the report arrives — the fog thickens through the aperture), the state's own site, the paper. The objects are apertures, not inventory.
2. **What do you say tonight, and to whom.** The Standing Order becomes **the Standing Word**: an act with a named person on it — your kids, your mother, the neighbor, your brother, the group chat. Loud (said to someone) or quiet (kept). That is a commitment with a cost citizens actually pay.
3. **What would change your mind.** Horvath's forecast, rephrased as the citizen's tool: name the falsifier. "Nothing would" is a legible posture and the mirror reads it warmly.
Then the record turns — the week of October 23–28, or the tape on the morning of January 3 — and lands on the falsifier. Then: **do you go back to the person you told?**

**What this dissolves.** The Georgia-chair call (§7.2) is moot: the player is at a kitchen table on the evening of January 2 while the senators announce; the tape drops next morning. Hitchcock keeps his suspense. The v7 class-representation order finally has a home; three homes hearing one broadcast never worked from the President's desk.

**What survives from v0.2:** the plates, newscast-then-paper, the address as the claim beat with the transcript floor, the forecast logic and the loud/quiet mirror, the recall tap, the river. **What goes:** pins-as-inventory, the six-unknown lattice, the presidential order menu, the 2021 office.

**Founder's UI rule for the paper (same turn):** the articles are fine at that size *if* you can tap one and open it larger. Do not overwhelm with context; make it openable and viewable. Use newspaper-like functions — a front page, an article page, turn the page, back to the front — not scrolling. The medium mimics the medium.

**Build:** two-scene alpha v0.3, "the citizen's chair." PROP 003 amended from The Standing Order to The Standing Word; PROP 002 (The Carry) withdrawn from this build and returned to the props room as a pattern for scenes where physical rationing is literal (a triage bay, a go-bag), never as a stand-in for attention.

### 15.2 PENDING founder ruling: the dinner question (2026-09-19)

**Founder:** *"I have a question about the fundamental dynamic of what would change your mind. Maybe what are your biggest questions? Can this adapt from what you chose? Gameplay direction feels uncertain."*

**The studio's read, recorded for the ruling.** The falsifier is Horvath's forecasting instrument carried over from the leader's chair (§10). It asks the player to predict an event from a fixed menu; the menu is authored per word, not per room, so it does not adapt to what the player saw, and nothing happens to it until Sunday. That is why the direction feels uncertain: the build has three half-verbs (pick rooms, say a word, name a falsifier) and the third does no work between nights.

**Recommendation: replace the falsifier with the question.** At dinner, after the Word: *What do you need to know?* A pool of three, drawn from the two rooms the player opened tonight (each aperture seeds one question) plus the standing question of the week, plus *Nothing. I know enough*, which is the certainty posture and is read warmly at the mirror as "Nothing would" was. The question is carried into the next night: the rooms that hold its answer say so only if the player goes there (the aperture line answers it, no marker on the door), the person you said the Word to asks it back ("You wanted to know if they were real. Are they?"), and the Sunday recall becomes "what did you need to know on Monday, and did you find out?" The replay lists each night's question, where the answer was, and whether you went there. The lever line's own-judgment mechanism (you went to the checkable thing on the night it became checkable) stops being an inference and becomes the game's verb. This is Schudson's monitorial citizen (retreat §7) as a mechanic: two rooms a night, a question in hand, choose where the answer might be.

**Tradeoffs.** About thirty question seeds (six rooms across five nights) and an answer map, text only, no new art. Risk of scavenger hunt if the door ever signals the answer; the rule is that it never does. Simpler alternative: keep the falsifier and draw its pool from tonight's rooms only, a data change that answers "can it adapt" without a new mechanic. More advanced: the question plus the kitchen table answering the cost questions and the person carrying the question across nights.

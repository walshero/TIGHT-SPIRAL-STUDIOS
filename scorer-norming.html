The Norming Table — where a "3" learns to mean the same thing.

Before a scoring team grades a single real artifact, it norms: everyone scores the same samples, compares, and talks until the rubric levels mean one thing across all of them. That agreement has a name — inter-rater reliability — and it's what makes the whole assessment trustworthy.

Why this is the whole game

Inter-rater reliability is the odds that two scorers, working alone, give the same artifact the same score. Low reliability means a student's result depends on who happened to grade them — which is not assessment, it's luck. Norming raises it before real scoring starts.

Exact agreement is the goal; adjacent agreement (within one level) is the working standard most teams accept. Two levels apart is the signal to stop and talk — usually the rubric language, not the artifact, is doing something ambiguous.

This is the same move Confluence runs at scale: norm the raters first, then let them score, then map the results to outcomes. Score well here and the report that comes out the other end means something.

The line, in one sentence

AI that extends your thinking is a tool; AI that replaces it is a trade — you get time back and give learning away. Neither is automatically "cheating." What makes it honest is (1) doing the thinking the assignment is actually trying to build in you, and (2) being able to say plainly what you used and how.

Your instructors are still working out their rules — the reports show most syllabi and assignments haven't said. Until they do, disclosing protects you from the exact fear students keep naming: being misread as dishonest when you weren't.

Where this comes from. The choices and "tells" are grounded in MassBay's own English department assessment reports (AY24-25 / 2025): students widely use GenAI (about 48% for ideas, 38% for language, 42% for editing tools) while most report it wasn't addressed in class, syllabus, or assignment; and in the department's academic-integrity indicators — fabricated citations, meta-textual AI chatter, and voice shifts between timed and revised writing.

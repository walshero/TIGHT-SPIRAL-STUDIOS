Score the room — read a delivery, not just a transcript.

A talk is more than its words. Here you read a short delivery — what was said, and how it was said (pace, pauses, eye contact, fillers) — and score it on the college's oral-communication rubric. Then you meet the normed score and see how close you landed.

No microphone, no recording, nothing stored. And it never scores accent or dialect — only the choices a speaker can change. That's the honest way to do this offline, and the fair way to do it at all.

Your calibration

How close were you to the rubric?

Scoring delivery is a skill you build the same way scorers do — by reading closely against the rubric and comparing. Here's where you landed. Every gap is a place to re-read the level language.

What "the room" is really measuring

Oral communication isn't performance or personality — the rubric asks a plain question: could the audience follow it, and were they moved? Organization, a clear central message, real supporting material, and delivery you can adjust — those are learnable moves, not fixed traits.

Because it scores described delivery, this tool never grades how someone sounds — no accent, no dialect, no first-language word choice treated as an error. It scores what a speaker can change. That's the whole point of assessing oral communication for equity instead of against it.

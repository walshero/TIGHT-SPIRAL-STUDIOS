## The 7 English Program Learning Outcomes (verbatim — stable across the reports)
> These are the **program** PLOs (Liberal Arts: English concentration) — distinct from the college's seven iSLOs.
1. Read and understand complex fiction and non-fiction texts from a variety of sources, including print, electronic, audio-visual, and other media.
2. Generate ideas for original texts and advance them through the drafting, revising, and editing process in order to produce polished final drafts.
3. Write focused, well-argued analyses and arguments that integrate personal experience and credible, external sources of information.
4. Collaborate effectively with others in the reading, writing, and editing process.
5. Apply rhetorical strategies to digital communication and multi-media texts, using a variety of open-access platforms. *(Drafted revision, not yet posted: "Use rhetorical strategies, effective design principles, and a variety of digital tools to produce meaningful multimodal texts.")*
6. Think in a creative, open-minded, information-based, culturally diverse, and critically analytical way, with increased awareness of the formative role language plays in the construction of identity and culture.
7. Understand the constantly evolving personal, professional, and civic landscape in which excellence in writing/language arts remains essential.

## The assessment arc (aggregate, by year)
- **AY24-25 / 2025 — SLO #2 (generate original ideas), read through GenAI.** Method: a *Perceptions of AI Misuse* survey during Fall 24/Spring 25 portfolio assessment; a common exit-exam essay on AI in higher ed; rubric language flagging AI-misuse as a pre-college-level indicator; a CopyLeaks detection pilot. **Findings (survey n = 118 of 134 EN101L portfolios):** 48.3% used AI to generate ideas, 38.1% to generate/refine language, 42.2% used Grammarly-type tools; **30%** said GenAI wasn't discussed in class, **60%** not in the syllabus, **75%** not in assignment instructions; 36% feared being misperceived as misusing AI; 15% had no understanding of GenAI, 44% strong. 18 incidents of suspected misuse flagged via the new tool. **Conclusions/actions:** streamline toward scoring the *exit exam* (context of assignment + readings) rather than the whole portfolio for non-ESL (random-sample EN101X; ESL continue 3 pieces); a more rigorous *in-person norming* session with async 48-hour scoring; PD on AI-resistant assignment design + a shared assignment repository on Teams; get a GenAI policy into every syllabus; recruit/compensate adjuncts to participate.
- **2021 — SLO #5 (digital/multimodal rhetoric).** Method: a semester-long website-building assignment scored on a Content / Navigation / Design / Writing-Mechanics / Voice rubric. **Finding:** students need more experience as *design thinkers* (user experience, flow); the outcome under-emphasizes design/typography/aesthetics → drafted the multimodal revision to SLO #5.
- **AY 2017-18 — SLO #3 (research / credible sources).** Context: college-wide rule that every program outcome be assessed at least once every 5 years ("so we'll double up"). Method: portfolio assessment; gather EN101/EN102 research-paper assignments; focus on locate/evaluate/integrate/cite credible sources.

## Recurring patterns (what the record teaches)
- **The method is portfolio assessment + norming/blind scoring** — a norming period, then blind reads, for ESL and EN101X. This is exactly what the studio's **Norming Table** (inter-rater reliability) and **Close the Loop** (per-dimension results → action) already model.
- **Adjunct participation** is a standing challenge; course-description changes and compensation are the levers tried.
- **Closing the loop is the chronic gap:** the SLO #5 revision was drafted (2021) and *still* not posted years later — the report itself names "the need … to close the loop by clearly posting and communicating all changes." The studio's Close the Loop instrument targets exactly this failure mode.
- **A ~5-year assessment cycle** (schedule table in AY24-25): 2024-25 = SLO #2; **2025-26 = SLO #1 (reading)**; remaining outcomes staggered across 2026-29.

## The co-req record (resolves the parked placement question)
In the department's own words (2025 report): **"As a result of long-term data on the efficacy of our co-req model, EN090 and EN098 have been officially retired."** The structure now:
- **EN101X** (English Composition I) + **EN101L** (co-requisite lab, same term, S/U, non-graduation credit) — "no longer a 2-semester developmental sequence."
- **Placement** into EN101-with-lab is via the College's placement process, **the English Department's Portfolio Assessment**, or completion of **ES150**.
This confirms MassBay as a genuine co-req pioneer and voids any "EN98-vs-EN101 place-out" framing. A future studio "placement/skill" instrument must be a **co-req support diagnostic** or a **portfolio-scoring skill-capture** (per SLO), never a remedial place-out.

The studio standard

Comfort is how you view content.

Font size, contrast, modes like warm and dark, motion stop, and screen reader options. Five things. The control is in the bottom corner, where a thumb already is, and it stays where you put it when you move to another page.

What changed in v3

Motion stop works. In v2 the rule that carried it was written as a selector list containing an @media block, which no CSS parser accepts, so the browser discarded it whole: zero of forty-seven rules survived. The button reported success and moved nothing, and the operating system's own reduced-motion setting went down with it. It is two separate rules now, and the comment above them says why they must never be merged again.

Comfort persists. Across a hundred and one surfaces, nothing remembered anything: every link followed put a reader who had chosen warm-dark back into daylight. The setting is stored locally and applied before the first paint, so there is no flash of the mode you did not ask for.

Clear Reader is back. It was the fifth stop of the original comfort control and the studio's only screen-reader-facing option, and it was deleted from forty-nine pages during a migration with no ruling recorded. It strips the design and leaves the document.

Font size no longer breaks the panel. The old size steps used zoom, which scales layout rather than type and does not appear in the arithmetic that positioned the panel, so at the largest setting the panel slid under the header by nine and a half pixels. Size is font-size now, multiplying whatever base your browser already uses rather than replacing it, and the panel measures the chrome instead of guessing at it.

What did not change

The colour work. Every pair that carries text clears the studio's seven-to-one target in all three modes, and warm-dark measures genuinely dark rather than dimmed. Tap targets were already well above the forty-eight pixel floor. Both were measured and both were left alone.

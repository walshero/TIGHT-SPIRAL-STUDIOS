## THE MOMENT THEY'RE ADVISING ON
v8 added a **social layer** — a reel of machines, a Ticket you hand to a person, and a **Maker score** ("did your bit carry the chain"). The single-player game was about an honest gap; the meta-machine adds *other people* and a *score against them.* That is the pivot these three were convened to judge. They arrive, independently, at the **same warning** — and it is about the score.

---

## JOHN CLEESE — protect open mode
*His creativity lecture: creativity lives in "open mode" — relaxed, playful, unhurried, unafraid of being wrong. "Closed mode" is anxious, purposeful, tunnel-visioned, humourless. You cannot be funny in closed mode.*

- **The read:** a comedy game whose headline is a *score* pushes the player into closed mode — optimising, anxious to win, afraid to be silly. The moment "how did I rank" is the loudest thing on screen, the play dies. You built a toy (the tap-the-machine chain) that is pure open mode; the score risks overwriting it.
- **Next step he'd push:** keep the **toy primary** and the score a quiet by-product. And expand the *creative* surface — let players **make their own bit** (author a gag, name a room), not just pick from four. A play-space, not a quiz. Reward the *making*, not the ranking.

## SHERRY TURKLE — connection, not "alone together"
*Alone Together / Reclaiming Conversation: technology promises connection and often delivers performance — we curate for an absent audience, and simulation substitutes for the messiness of real relation.*

- **The read:** the **Ticket is a genuinely good instinct** — it is not a feed or a global leaderboard; it is a thing you hand to *one specific person*, human to human, like passing a note. That is the anti-"alone together" design. But the **aggregate Maker score pulls the other way** — turn the crowd into a metric and you're performing for an audience, not connecting with a friend.
- **Next step she'd push:** build the **reciprocal exchange**, not the aggregate. Not a bigger reel, not a scoreboard — *you send a machine to one person, they send one back, and you each see how your rooms differ.* That is a **conversation** (my sense of a room vs. yours), which is connection. The score is the flight *from* conversation.

## AGNES HORVATH — keep the liminal generative, not permanent
*Liminality is the threshold — the in-between where transformation and creativity happen. But made **permanent**, it degrades into **trickster logic**: imitation spirals, cynicism, gaming, chasing what mobilises the crowd. Modernity, she argues, universalises trickster logic.*

- **The read:** the game's soul is a *liminal* one — comedy lives in the pause, and the mechanic is literally **the gap** (your guess vs. the room). That is the *generative* liminal, and it's good. The danger is the score making the liminal **permanent**: an endless comparison spiral where players stop asking "what's funny" and start asking "what *wins*" — **the trickster move, and the exact failure the game already indicts** ("a lazy poll gives a comforting number that isn't true"). A social score can quietly turn the whole game into optimising the crowd — cynicism dressed as calibration.
- **Next step she'd push:** design the **exit** from the liminal. A round must *resolve* — leave the player with **a made thing** (a machine they're proud of, a keepsake), not just a rank that yanks them back to compare again. And never let the crowd become a **trickster-mirror** you chase. Completion over comparison.

---

## WHERE THEY CONVERGE (the one warning)
> **The social *score* is the risk. Cleese (it forces closed mode), Turkle (it makes you perform, "alone together"), and Horvath (it makes the liminal permanent — the trickster spiral) all say the same thing from three doors: a comparison score turns *play* into optimisation, *connection* into performance, and the *generative gap* into a cynical loop. The game's soul is the play and the person; the score endangers both.**

And note: this is the game's **own thesis turned on the game** — it warns against lazy polling that chases a comforting crowd-number, and a Maker leaderboard would *be* that.

## THE DECISIVE NEXT STEP (synthesis)
**Do not scale the social (no bigger reel, no leaderboard, no taste-landscape yet). Build the two-person reciprocal loop, and demote the aggregate score to a whisper.**

Concretely, the next increment:
1. **The reply.** A Ticket you receive can be **answered** — you send one back. The screen that matters becomes *"here's how you and `<friend>` each read the same room,"* a side-by-side of two humans' senses of funny. Connection (Turkle), low-stakes play (Cleese), a resolving exchange that ends (Horvath).
2. **Demote the Maker score** from a headline number to a quiet line inside that exchange — a by-product of play, never the point.
3. **Then** (Cleese's larger frontier, later): let players **author a bit** — the creative surface that keeps it open mode.
4. **Guard** (Horvath, standing): every round ends in *a made thing*, not a rank; the crowd is never something to optimise toward. Keep the reads honest, or the game becomes the trickster it warns about.

The taste-landscape / norming payoff in the design doc is still real — but it's the *aggregate*, and all three say: earn the **person-to-person** first, or the aggregate becomes the thing that hollows it out.

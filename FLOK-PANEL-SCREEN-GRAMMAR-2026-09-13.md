# Flok · screen grammar panel — design/learning alignment

**2026-09-13 · convened inline (no fleet, per the cost ruling) · seats: the standing six
from the CYL bible — Turkle · Horvath · Gitlin · Filament · FableVision · Social-Class
Historian. Lead seat: Horvath, because the question is measurement.**

The founder's ask: *"screen grammar to maximize design and learning alignment."* Not a
skin pass. Whether the way the screen is arranged teaches the thing the game is about.

---

## HORVATH — two constructs, never blurred. RULED, IMPLEMENTED.

The measurement constitution already adopted for Choose Your Leader names exactly two
constructs and forbids blurring them. Flok has both and was painting them identically.

| | construct | Flok's version | status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **THE DESCENT** — where it pulled *you*. **Measured.** | taps · rewards · screens scrolled · alerts fired | real counts of the player |
| 2 | **THE DISPLACEMENT** — what the words do vs where the policy lands. **Authored, shown, never scored on the player.** | the engagement meter · the cohort · the rank ladder | authored fiction |

**The violation, visible on the last screen.** Both constructs rendered in `--phos-ink`
teal, tabular numerals, identical panel cards. The screen that says *"it measured you"*
was dressed exactly like the screen that measured the cohort. A player cannot tell which
number is about them. Two of the four rows also carried `.warn` amber with no stated
rule — a grammar with no grammar.

**Ruled.** Teal is the machine's colour and nothing else. Your numbers are `--ink`, plain
and uncelebrated. The two languages meet **once**, at the turn.

## HORVATH — one source of truth: the lesson is the ratio. RULED, IMPLEMENTED.

`14` and `3` sat in two unrelated boxes. The variable-ratio schedule — the entire thing
lever 1 teaches, and the reason Skinner is cited on its research card — lives in the
*relationship* between those numbers, and the screen had thrown the relationship away.
The player was shown their own conditioning as trivia.

One card now holds both, with the schedule named in the player's own numbers:

> You tapped Boost **14** · It paid off **3**
> *About one in 5. That is the schedule you were taught to build.*

Zero-reward path handled: *"It never paid off. You kept tapping anyway."* — which is the
stronger reading and the one a careless player will hit.

## FILAMENT / FABLEVISION — one screen, one job. RULED, IMPLEMENTED.

Applied to this panel's own first output: amber was reserved as the colour the two
constructs collapse in, then used three times on one screen (the glyph, the ratio, the
turn). The turn is the screen's job. Glyph demoted to `--dim`, ratio to `--ink`, amber
now appears exactly once — on *"The cohort was people like you."*

## GITLIN — deferred, not dismissed.

Gitlin's lens is saturation: the torrent is the medium, and Flok paces itself as five
discrete lessons behind a Next button, which is the opposite of the thing it depicts. A
real reading. **But the studio has already ruled Horvath over Gitlin twice** (CYL R4, R12),
and a paced lesson is what makes the measurement legible. Recorded and deferred, not
adopted.

## TURKLE — OPEN, needs the founder. NOT IMPLEMENTED.

Lever 5 turns named people with faces — `north_trail`, `q.ramos`, `daily.loaf` — into a
reply-rate percentage. That reduction *is* the lesson. But the ending then says **"the
cohort"**, an abstraction, at the exact moment the game wants the player to feel a person.

Turkle's move: name one. *"The cohort was people like you"* → the same turn with one of
the feed's own names carried through to the end, so the person the player throttled comes
back with a face.

**Not implemented because the turn is the founder's voice**, and this is the game's single
most important line. The panel recommends it; the founder writes it.

## SOCIAL-CLASS HISTORIAN — OPEN, out of this pass.

The cohort has no class. Ten invented handles posting about basil, trail runs and sourdough
is one demographic wearing ten names, and "people like you" quietly means people like the
player. Naming it so it is not lost.

## Standing after this pass

   it fits one phone screen. It is the main play surface and it is the top open item.

   single-file-offline law makes embedding them a real size decision.
3. **The feed is ten hardcoded posts.** The illusion breaks on the scroll lever, which is
   the one lever whose whole point is that it never ends.

5. **The cohort's class** — Social-Class Historian seat, unscheduled.

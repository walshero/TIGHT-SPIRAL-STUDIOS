## 1. The thesis

The speech works high. The policy lands low. On October 22, November 4 at midnight, November 3 at 9:32, the set carried freedom, honor, a people, a we — the top of the pyramid. That same week, the actual decisions arrived at the base: a draft card by the sugar bowl, a grocery shelf stripped bare, a reservist's paycheck cut off overnight, a birthday about to become a number in a drum. The broadcast is not a lie, mostly. It is a lens that only points up. The gap between where the words aim and where the policy hits a kitchen — that is the Maslow Hierarchy Effect, and it is measurable: the words operated three rungs above where the house lived. The player's job is unchanged — you judge what you were allowed to see — but now the record turns twice: what was hidden, and who paid. Naming that distance is the win. Not cynicism. Calibration.

## 3. The Maslow instrument spec *(Horvath — measurement discipline)*

**FOUNDER RULING (2026-07-30): Horvath's position adopted — the instrument ships as the WORDS | LIFE two-column, not the one-ladder Split Pyramid merge. Plainer screen, lower clutter, measurement integrity first. The pyramid stays in this document as the considered-and-declined alternative.**


**Displacement is a property of the artifact. It is coded at authoring time. It is never a property of the player.** The player is never assigned a Maslow level. The game computes a number about the message, from sourced text and sourced effects.

### 3.1 The Register/Impact Codebook

Two fixed codes ship inside each scene's data, produced before any player touches the build.

- **RHETORIC REGISTER.** Unitize the broadcast quote into clauses. Each clause codes to one of five need-domains via a written decision tree with anchor exemplars: L1 body/food/shelter · L2 safety/survival · L3 belonging/us-we/a-people · L4 standing/honor/esteem · L5 ideals/destiny/who-we-could-be. Example: "the great silent majority" codes L3; "peace with honor" codes L4/L5; almost nothing in the Nov 3 address codes L1-L2.
- **POLICY IMPACT.** Each group card's documented effect codes to the level where it lands in a household: the draft lottery lands L1/L2; casualty notification lands L1/L2; a bargained-down convention seat lands L4.
- **DISPLACEMENT.** `D = register level − impact level`, per scene, per group. Stated in the game in words and a number.

### 3.2 The checks (a rule that can't be a check is a wish)

1. **Dual coding, gated by script.** Two independent coding passes on every scene. A small script computes agreement (coding sheet in, Krippendorff's alpha out) and **exits 1 below 0.70** — same shape as `preship-contrast-gate.py`. Disagreements adjudicated by the founder against the source text. One AI session is one rater; single-rater coding is not defensible coding.
2. **Order lock, asserted by script.** Sequence: Beat 1 blind ask → Beat 2a/2b + identical post ask → Beat 2.5 → Beat 3. **No Maslow placement UI ever precedes the post-record trust ask.** A preship script asserts DOM/beat order and exits 1 on violation. If a placement task precedes the post ask, the trust delta is contaminated by an induced analytical frame and all prior descent data becomes incomparable.
3. **Visual separation or joint sign-off.** The build contains two Maslow-shaped constructs: the descent (where rhetoric pulled YOU; ipsative; trauma-railed) and the displacement (where the WORDS play vs where the POLICY lands; artifact-coded). The panel's convergent design merges them onto one ladder with three shape-and-label-distinct marks (§2). Horvath's firewall stands: that merge ships only on a founder + Measurement Design joint sign-off. The fallback is his two parallel columns — WORDS | LIFE — joined by a horizontal arrow of length D, with its own icon set and no shared color tokens with the descent ladder.
4. **Shown, not scored, in v5.** Hard cap at ship: the player's measured signal remains exactly the two identical-wording asks and their ipsative delta. No third ask, no policy-comprehension check, no group-empathy meter, no displacement term in the descent math. The RHETORIC/POLICY marks are authored content the instrument sits inside. If the marks ever move on player input, that is an instrument change and triggers the gate.

### 3.3 Phase 2: the placement task *(enters through the gate, not through a patch)*

When the studio is ready to score displacement-noticing: after the post-record ask, two placements — "Where do these WORDS live?" and "Where did this land in a kitchen, a draft board, a rent envelope?" Big discrete labeled buttons, keyboard-reachable, 44px+, one decision per screen (this settles the v5 spec's open buttons-vs-slider question for this instrument: buttons). Immediate feedback with evidence: "here is where two coders placed it, and why" — the highlighted phrases, the sourced group card. Prediction checked against evidence, never a grade against an oracle.

**Scoring is sensitivity, not hit-rate.** Per-item signed placement error against the codebook; across the session, a d-prime-style discrimination read — did the player name displacement when D was large AND withhold it when D was small? This requires the roster to carry **at least one genuinely aligned, low-D item** — a scene or card where the rhetoric's register and the policy's landing substantially meet. Candidate: within JFK Oct 22 1962, the quarantine announcement paired safety-register words with a same-day safety-level action — founder + fact-check to VERIFY and pick. The Filament seat's Aligned Household is the same control item in household form (candidate: a defense-industry or base-town household where mobilization meant paychecks and overtime — VERIFY per scene before any specific claim ships). Without the aligned item, "always predict betrayal" scores perfectly and the instrument trains cynicism. Arc copy reports "gaps you named / alignments you recognized," never an accuracy percentage.

### 3.4 The framing disclosure

Maslow's hierarchy is a taxonomy here, not a validated ordering. The strict-stages reading is empirically weak and a skeptical faculty reviewer will know it. The five levels are coded need-domains with a height metaphor. The Glass Engine's deep ring says so plainly, or the whole instrument is dismissible on its framing.

## 4. The Groups-of-America system

**Who the player meets.** Per scene, drawn from the dossiers (§6): 1962 — the recalled reservist's household, the quarantine-line sailor's family, the family inside the announced range arc with nothing but a shelter sign, the Black family the housing order left out, the Cuban-American family in Miami cut off from the island. 1964 — the draft-age son's kitchen without a deferment, the Seventh Fleet family, the poor family the poverty act was written for, the Mississippi families whose dead were pulled from a dam the same day. 1969 — the household with a birthday about to enter the drum, the 2-S household that holds a shelter its neighbors don't, the Black families whose government argued in court against their children that same autumn, the AFDC household promised a floor that never arrived, the Cambodian villages that sit outside the frame entirely — tagged "outside the frame," not on the ladder, because the frame's edge is the point.

**How impact renders — the dignity rules (hard, art-checklist enforced, not taste calls):**

1. **Objects and documents, never faces.** The withheld-figure rule extends to households: a lit window, a table, hands, a card, a calendar, a suitcase. Never a face, never a name, never dialect or costume shorthand. The leader is withheld so you judge the message; the household is specified-but-faceless so you feel the landing without being told whom to pity. *(Hitchcock, Filament, Turkle — convergent.)*
2. **Coping, never collapse.** Every household shows competence and order — a list, a packed case, a mended thing. No weeping, no squalor, no bodies, no gore. Harm enters as a document. The telegram envelope is never opened. Despair-porn is a Trauma HALT. *(FableVision.)*
3. **Third person, dated, sourced.** Every "this landed on these people" line is record, not role-assignment. The game never implies a present-day player carries or owes that experience. *(Gitlin trauma rail.)*
4. **Composite, labeled.** Households are composites grounded in verified demographics, captioned visibly: "This family is a composite drawn from documented accounts. The notice on their table is real." No composite person presented as fact; no real family used without consent — a real family's story as "typical household" is a worse failure than a fabricated one. *(Filament, Horvath.)*
5. **The evidence standard.** Every group card: GROUP (named plainly) / DOCUMENTED EFFECT (dated) / IMPACT LEVEL (codebook) / SOURCE STRING (`data-art-*` schema) / QUANTIFIER where sourceable. Any card lacking a solid source carries VERIFY and is excluded from play by the same mechanism that gates living presidents: `PLAY = cards.filter(not gated)`. *(Horvath.)*
6. **Miss copy lands on the broadcast, never the player.** "The broadcast didn't show you this house" — never "you didn't see them." *(Filament, all rails.)*
7. **Named reviewers, not intentions.** The 1964 and 1969 households depict working-class and Black families bearing disproportionate burden. A sensitivity/cultural-review pass by a real reader, logged like the Measurement bench, is required before those households ship. *(FableVision.)*
8. **Military-connected players are in the room today.** The game judges rhetoric and concealment, never service or service members. One wrong sentence converts a media-literacy lesson into an attack on a player's parent. The skippable content note extends to the household scenes. *(FableVision.)*
9. **The player's own chair is one of the houses.** In any multi-window view, one window is always yours — groups are never specimens behind glass. *(Filament.)*
10. **Same Light, Other Rooms** *(Turkle, phase 2 with the Block Walk)*: the pull-back to a street of identical blue-gray windows — the largest "we" ever assembled, assembled out of rooms that could not see each other. Each tappable window is a lit window and a dated fact pair: what the broadcast said to this room / what arrived at this room's door. "One voice. Millions of rooms. Each alone." Audience figure shown with source (JFK Oct 22 1962 commonly estimated near 100 million viewers/listeners — VERIFY before ship).

## 5. The coverup/displacement layer *(Gitlin)*

Frames work by selection, emphasis, and exclusion. The layer has four moves, all built on artifacts the player personally handled:

1. **The Preemption Cut (Beat -1).** The evening's real TV listing grays out; the interrupt slate takes the screen, paired with the set-click (sound never sole carrier). One keypress to stay with the channel — the only option, and the game says so: "There was no other channel to turn to." The attention monopoly, felt before any quote is read. Slate is visible, not flicker-only; no strobe. Listings and address times VERIFY against period TV pages (Oct 22 1962 7:00pm ET slot; Aug 4 1964 ~11:36pm ET; Nov 3 1969 9:32pm ET).
2. **The stamped re-read (Beat 2a).** One or two prelude cards the player flipped past as neutral background truth return identical, now stamped: what the newsroom could not have known, and the later document that showed it (NSA 2005 Tonkin study; the Menu revelations). The reversal indicts the frame the player was handed, not the player. Zero new assets. Candidate cards: an Aug 5 1964 front page reporting the "second attack" as fact (VERIFY masthead and licensing); Nov 1969 withdrawal coverage with no Cambodia (VERIFY).
3. **The Morning-After Front Page (Beat 2.5 Panel B).** The finite attention budget as arithmetic: the address's share of page one numerically labeled ("The address: 71% of page one" — VERIFY per chosen paper), displaced stories below the fold, tappable, expanding to full-contrast text. The top of the page holds the top of the pyramid; the base of the pyramid is literally below the fold. Real scans where licensable; otherwise real verbatim headlines re-set in the game's neutral layout, explicitly labeled "headlines re-set from [source, date]." Never a fabricated headline.
4. **The Bumped Ledger (arc screen).** The frames you caught, listed with group and rung tags, closed by the structural sentence — three networks, wire services, news routines: a system, not a single hand. Deepest ring: "This game also measured whether you looked below the fold," count shown descriptively. No grade, no percent-missed.

**The intent line (hard).** "The address ate 71% of page one" is arithmetic. "The story was buried on purpose" is an intent claim that usually cannot be proven. In this corpus only the Cambodia falsified-records case is documented coverup intent (1973 Senate hearings); the Jupiter trade is documented concealment of a settlement; everything else ships as measured displacement. Copy standard is Gitlin's own position: frames as routine practice, not plot. Violating this teaches conspiracy-thinking and breaks the calibration floor.

### Scene 1 — JFK, Monday, October 22, 1962, 7:00 p.m. Eastern

A 17-minute address from the Oval Office, carried simultaneously on the television and radio networks — one message, no counter-programming, among the largest audiences for any presidential address to that date (exact audience figure: VERIFY). The frame the audience could not see: U-2 photographs of the missile sites were taken October 14 and briefed to Kennedy on October 16; the calm "within the past week" address was the product of six days of secret ExComm deliberation in which airstrike and invasion were argued to the edge — and it arrived into a country that, three weeks earlier, had watched federal troops put down a segregationist riot at Ole Miss, and that was eighteen days from a covert-war directive it would not learn about for years. The rhetoric operated at the top of the pyramid — freedom, national character, the willingness to pay any cost. The policy landed at the base: sailors on a quarantine line, reservists recalled overnight from civilian jobs, bomber crews at DEFCON 2, Black families still shut out of most of the housing market, and a secret trade that ended the crisis but was hidden from the voters who went to the polls fifteen days later.

**Broadcast claims:**

1. "Within the past week, unmistakable evidence has established" that the Soviets are building offensive missile sites on "that imprisoned island" — missiles capable of striking Washington D.C., the Panama Canal, Cape Canaveral, or Mexico City. (The frame implies a days-old discovery and an immediate response; the photographs were eight days old and the response was six days deliberated.)
2. The United States is initiating "a strict quarantine on all offensive military equipment under shipment to Cuba" — deliberately not the word "blockade," which is an act of war in international law.
3. Any nuclear missile launched from Cuba against any nation in the Western Hemisphere will be regarded "as an attack by the Soviet Union on the United States, requiring a full retaliatory response upon the Soviet Union."
4. The deception is entirely Soviet: Gromyko's assurances that the buildup was defensive were "false" — the United States is presented as purely reactive, its own hands clean of provocation.
5. "The cost of freedom is always high — and Americans have always paid it"; "many months of sacrifice and self-discipline lie ahead." An appeal to national character and belonging — the cost named abstractly, never as whose body, whose paycheck, whose draft notice.

#### Decision 1.1 — Operation Mongoose escalation directive *(confidence: verified)*

- **Date:** 1962-10-04 (program authorized 1961-11-30)
- **Decision:** The Special Group (Augmented), chaired by Robert Kennedy, orders "more dynamic action" in the covert war on Cuba: expanded sabotage, plans for mining Cuban harbors, and schemes to capture Cuban personnel for interrogation, accepting that "a very considerable amount of attribution and noise must be expected." The program itself had run since November 30, 1961.
- **What it did:** Eighteen days before the address, the administration escalated an active, secret campaign of sabotage and destabilization against Cuba — one of the reasons Havana and Moscow wanted the missiles there at all. The October 22 broadcast presented the missile deployment as unprovoked aggression against a blameless United States.
- **Groups:**
  - Cuban exile operatives recruited by the CIA in South Florida for infiltration and sabotage runs — sent on covert raids with deniability built in; their risk was the "noise" the directive priced in, and their existence was unspeakable on the broadcast. *(Maslow: safety)*
  - Cuban-American families in Miami with relatives still on the island — the covert war and then the crisis hardened the severing of family ties; contact, travel, and remittances closed down around them. *(Maslow: belonging)*
  - The general television audience of October 22 — asked to judge Soviet deception and commit to sacrifice without being told their own government was running a sabotage war against Cuba; their civic judgment was exercised on a curated record. *(Maslow: esteem)*
- **Media note:** The address's moral architecture — "that imprisoned island," Soviet lies, American restraint — required the covert war not to exist on screen. The rhetoric of clean-hands defense displaced the base-level fact of an ongoing U.S. sabotage campaign; the audience's blind trust rating was taken inside that omission.
- **Sources:** Memorandum of Mongoose Meeting, October 4, 1962 — Avalon Project, Yale Law School (FRUS 1961-63, Vol. XI) · Operation Mongoose — GlobalSecurity.org · Memorandum for the Record, October 16, 1962 (RFK dissatisfaction, daily Mongoose meetings) — Avalon Project, Yale Law School

#### Decision 1.2 — Proclamation 3504, the quarantine *(confidence: verified)*

- **Date:** 1962-10-23 (effective 1962-10-24)
- **Decision:** Kennedy signs Proclamation 3504, "Interdiction of the Delivery of Offensive Weapons to Cuba," at 7:05 p.m. October 23, effective the morning of October 24, after securing OAS authorization; the Navy establishes an armed interception line around Cuba.
- **What it did:** Put U.S. warships on an interception line with orders to stop, board, and if necessary disable vessels bound for Cuba — a blockade in everything but name, at the edge of an act of war, enforced by crews with live weapons against Soviet-chartered shipping while the world watched to see who would flinch.
- **Groups:**
  - Navy sailors and aircrews enforcing the quarantine line in the Caribbean — placed at the exact point of first contact between the superpowers; any boarding gone wrong was the war; on October 27 U.S. ships were depth-charging near a Soviet submarine they did not know was nuclear-armed. *(Maslow: safety)*
  - Wives and children of quarantine-line and fleet crews — their husbands and fathers sailed toward the confrontation the president had just described as possibly costing everything, with no timetable and censored operational news. *(Maslow: safety)*
  - Cuban-American communities in Florida — the quarantine formalized the wall between them and the island; goods, mail, and movement to family in Cuba choked off as their home region became a military staging ground. *(Maslow: belonging)*
- **Media note:** The single word "quarantine" is the media strategy: public-health language for a naval blockade, chosen because "blockade" is legally an act of war. The broadcast's soft noun kept the action sounding defensive and limited while armed interception began at sea — the wording displaced the escalation it named.
- **Sources:** Proclamation 3504: Interdiction of the Delivery of Offensive Weapons to Cuba, 23 October 1962 — JFK Presidential Library (JFKPOF-041-019) · Proclamation 3504 — The American Presidency Project, UC Santa Barbara · Proclamation signing photograph, 7:05 p.m. October 23, 1962 — JFK Presidential Library

#### Decision 1.3 — Maximum military mobilization *(confidence: verified)*

- **Date:** 1962-10-22 to 1962-10-28 (call-up authority 1962-10-03)
- **Decision:** SAC moves to DEFCON 3 on October 22 and to DEFCON 2 on October 24 — the only confirmed time SAC ever reached it — with roughly sixty nuclear-armed B-52s on continuous airborne alert; under Public Law 87-736 (passed October 3, authorizing call-up of 150,000 Ready Reservists) and Executive Order 11058, Kennedy orders 14,200 Air Force Reservists — eight troop carrier wings and six aerial port squadrons — to active duty on the evening of October 27 for a possible invasion airlift; they report the morning of October 28 and are released by November 28.
- **What it did:** Converted the address's abstract "sacrifice" into named bodies within days: bomber and missile crews held one step from nuclear war, civilian reservists pulled overnight from jobs and families to fly an invasion that was actively being planned, and invasion forces staged into Florida.
- **Groups:**
  - The 14,200 Air Force Reservists recalled on the night of October 27-28, and their households — overnight severance from civilian jobs, income, and family on twelve-month orders, to crew the airlift for an invasion of Cuba; the address's "months of sacrifice" arriving as their specific months. *(Maslow: physiological)*
  - SAC bomber, tanker, and missile crews at DEFCON 2 — held on airborne and ground alert with live thermonuclear weapons, one order from execution; the personal floor of the "full retaliatory response" the broadcast promised. *(Maslow: safety)*
  - Draft-eligible men without deferments — the peacetime draft was running, invasion plans required infantry, and the reserve call-up signaled which bodies would be next if the quarantine failed. *(Maslow: safety)*
  - Households inside the missiles' announced range arc (the Southeast, cities named in the address) — told on live television they were targetable within minutes, then offered nothing actionable; the civil defense program amounted to fallout-shelter signs on buildings, not evacuation or protection. *(Maslow: physiological)*
- **Media note:** The broadcast asked for "patience," "sacrifice," and "self-discipline" — esteem-and-belonging language — while the operational reality it did not itemize was DEFCON 2, an invasion airlift crewed by recalled civilians, and a public whose entire protection was a yellow-and-black sign in a basement. The cost of freedom was named as national character; it was paid as paychecks, alert rotations, and bodies staged in Florida.
- **Sources:** Involuntary Reserve Activations for U.S. Military Operations Since World War II — Congressional Research Service (RL30637) · Executive Order 11058 — The American Presidency Project, UC Santa Barbara · 1962 — Cuban Missile Crisis Fact Sheet — Air Force Historical Support Division · Cuban Missile Crisis: Consensus, Announcement and DEFCON-2 — Defense Media Network · October 27, 1962 — JFK Presidential Library, Cuban Missile Crisis microsite

#### Decision 1.4 — The secret Jupiter missile trade *(confidence: verified)*

- **Date:** 1962-10-27 (withdrawal completed by April 1963)
- **Decision:** Robert Kennedy tells Soviet Ambassador Dobrynin on the evening of October 27 that U.S. Jupiter missiles in Turkey will be withdrawn within four to five months, on condition the assurance never be made public; the missiles come out of Turkey (and Italy) by April 1963; the administration publicly denies any deal, most of ExComm and NATO are kept ignorant, and RFK's posthumous memoir Thirteen Days is edited to obscure the trade, which is not fully documented until the 1980s.

The policy that actually resolved the missile threat (a mutual withdrawal) was withheld from the public, the Congress, the allies, and the history books for a generation, so the rhetoric's lesson could survive contact with the record.

### Scene 2 — LBJ, August 4, 1964, 11:36 p.m. EDT

The regular program has just stopped. The room is a year heavier than 1962: near midnight, colder black-and-white, a lived-in reading chair, the set the only light. A grave silhouette behind a podium-shape, two flag-shapes, a bare wall. The signal feels a hair less certain than it should — which is the scene's whole truth, because the attack the address leans on hardest most likely never happened. The gap this scene teaches: the broadcast operated at the top of the pyramid — high seas, freedom, peace, no wider war, national unity at midnight — while the real decisions of that same month landed at the base: the body of the draft-age man without a deferment, the pilot shot down the next morning, the poor family the poverty act was written for, and the three bodies pulled from an earthen dam in Mississippi that very day, whose story the midnight broadcast displaced from the nation's screens.

**Broadcast claims:**

1. Renewed hostile actions against United States ships "on the high seas" in the Gulf of Tonkin have today required me to order military action in reply — framing the destroyers as innocent vessels attacked in international waters.
2. The initial attack on the destroyer Maddox on August 2 "was repeated today" by hostile vessels attacking two U.S. destroyers with torpedoes — asserting the disputed second attack as established fact, hours after the on-scene commander had cabled doubts.
3. "Air action is now in execution" against North Vietnamese gunboats and supporting facilities — retaliation announced as already underway, and characterized as "limited and fitting."
4. "We still seek no wider war" — the address's emotional center: restraint, gravity, a father's calm at midnight.
5. He will ask Congress for a resolution making it clear the government is "united in its determination to take all necessary measures in support of freedom and in defense of peace in southeast Asia" — unity language wrapped around what became a blank check.

#### Decision 2.1 — OPLAN 34A covert raids and DESOTO patrols *(confidence: verified)*

- **Date:** Approved January 1964; raids from February 1964; South Vietnamese commando raids on Hon Me and Hon Ngu islands July 30-31, 1964, with USS Maddox on a DESOTO signals-intelligence patrol in the same waters
- **What it did:** The U.S. ran and supported covert maritime raids against North Vietnamese coastal targets while destroyers gathered signals intelligence offshore. North Vietnam read the Maddox as connected to the July 30-31 island raids; the August 2 attack on the Maddox came in that context. The public was told nothing about 34A — the attacks were presented as unprovoked aggression against routine patrols on the high seas.
- **Groups:**
  - Sailors aboard USS Maddox and USS Turner Joy — placed in contested waters adjacent to covert raids they did not control, then fired upon on August 2; their bodies were the point of contact between a secret policy and a public story. *(Maslow: safety)*
  - South Vietnamese commandos running the 34A coastal raids — carried out high-casualty covert operations the American public never heard of; many were captured or killed. *(Maslow: physiological)*
  - American voters in the 1964 election season — denied the operational context needed to judge the "unprovoked attack" claim; the raw material of self-governing judgment was withheld. *(Maslow: self-actualization)*
- **Media note:** The broadcast's frame — innocent ships, high seas, unprovoked aggression — displaced the covert-raid context entirely. Nothing in the address acknowledged that U.S.-supported commandos had raided North Vietnamese islands days earlier, or that the Maddox was an intelligence platform. The rhetoric ran on national honor and open seas; the withheld fact was an ongoing covert war.

#### Decision 2.2 — Retaliation ordered on a disputed attack: Operation Pierce Arrow *(confidence: verified)*

- **Date:** Ordered August 4, 1964 (announced in the midnight address); flown August 5, 1964 — roughly 64 sorties against torpedo-boat bases and the Vinh oil storage facility
- **What it did:** The first overt U.S. airstrikes against North Vietnam, launched in reprisal for the August 4 "second attack." That attack almost certainly never happened: on-scene commander Capt. John Herrick cabled within hours that "freak weather effects" and an "overeager sonarman" made the reports doubtful and urged "complete evaluation before any further action taken." The NSA's own declassified 2005 study (Robert Hanyok, "Skunks, Bogies, Silent Hounds, and the Flying Fish") concluded no attack occurred on August 4 and that signals intelligence was mishandled and skewed to support the attack claim. Lt. (j.g.) Richard Sather was killed; Lt. (j.g.) Everett Alvarez was shot down and became the first U.S. aviator held as a POW in North Vietnam — for over eight years.
- **Groups:**
  - Navy carrier pilots flying from USS Ticonderoga and USS Constellation — sent against defended targets in reprisal for an event that likely never occurred: Sather killed, Alvarez captured and imprisoned eight-plus years; the first bodies of the air war. *(Maslow: physiological)*
  - Families of servicemen already deployed with the Seventh Fleet — learned from a midnight broadcast that their sons and husbands were now in a shooting war; the address reached them as reassurance while the risk to their people escalated. *(Maslow: safety)*
  - North Vietnamese civilians and workers near the Vinh oil facility and coastal bases — on the receiving end of the first U.S. strikes of the war, justified by an attack the striking government's own intelligence later concluded did not happen. *(Maslow: physiological)*
- **Media note:** The address asserted the second attack as certain fact ("was repeated today") at the very hours the on-scene commander was cabling doubt up the chain. The broadcast's frame — measured reprisal for confirmed aggression — covered the reality that the trigger was unverified and, per the NSA's later study, nonexistent. The rhetoric was restraint; the policy was the first strikes of an air war launched on bad evidence.

#### Decision 2.3 — The Gulf of Tonkin Resolution: a pre-drafted blank check *(confidence: verified)*

- **Date:** Passed August 7, 1964 (House 416-0; Senate 88-2, Senators Wayne Morse and Ernest Gruening dissenting); signed August 10, 1964. Resolution language had been drafted months earlier, in late May-June 1964, by Assistant Secretary of State William Bundy
- **What it did:** Authorized the President "to take all necessary measures to repel any armed attack against the forces of the United States and to prevent further aggression" — with no time limit, no geographic limit, and no declaration of war. The escalation authority reached deepest into the lives of men with the fewest shields: college deferments protected the enrolled, so the draft and the combat arms drew disproportionately on working-class, poor, Black, and rural young men (Christian Appy's Working-Class War documents that roughly 80 percent of enlisted men came from working-class or poor backgrounds).
- **Groups:**
  - Draft-eligible men without college deferments — disproportionately working-class, poor, and rural — the resolution was the legal instrument that would put their bodies in the war; deferment policy meant the burden ran down the class ladder. *(Maslow: physiological)*
  - Black and Latino young men channeled into combat units in the early escalation years — bore disproportionate combat exposure and casualties as the escalation the resolution authorized unfolded. *(Maslow: physiological)*
  - Voters in the November 1964 election weighing Johnson as the "peace candidate" against Goldwater — cast ballots without knowing the war-expansion instrument had been drafted before the incident used to pass it; consent given inside a managed frame. *(Maslow: self-actualization)*
  - Families of servicemen already in or bound for Southeast Asia — the "no wider war" assurance was the message they received; the open-ended escalation authority was the policy they lived under. *(Maslow: safety)*
- **Media note:** "We still seek no wider war" framed the resolution as unity-signaling restraint. The withheld facts: the blank-check text predated the incident by months, waiting for an occasion, and Congress debated it for roughly two days on the administration's version of events. The broadcast's top-of-pyramid language — freedom, peace, unity — covered the base-of-pyramid transaction: authority over the bodies of draft-age men, transferred without a declaration of war.

### Scene 3 — Nixon, Monday, November 3, 1969, 9:32 p.m. Eastern

The Silent Majority / Vietnamization address, delivered live from the White House to an audience estimated at over 70 million. The room per the period bible: warm, faded, worn at the corners — Ektachrome gone amber. The set carries the first uneasy color, flesh tones bleeding at the edges. He looks straight out of the set, right at the room — the most intimate rhetoric of the three scenes, which is why the withheld figure matters most here. Everything the audience is allowed to see is on that screen. What is not on the screen: a neutral country under falsified-paperwork bombardment for seven months, a birthday lottery four weeks out, a Supreme Court ruling from five days earlier that the administration's own lawyers had argued against, and a poverty floor promised in August on this same set that will never arrive.

**Broadcast claims:**

1. I have a plan to end the war: Vietnamization. We have adopted a plan, worked out with the South Vietnamese, for the complete withdrawal of all U.S. combat ground forces on an orderly timetable — but "I have not and do not intend to announce the timetable." (The plan is asserted; the schedule is withheld.)
2. I have exhausted the peaceful road: "I have explored every possible private avenue that might lead to a settlement" — public forums, private channels, secret initiatives, including a personal letter to Ho Chi Minh in mid-July 1969, answered three days before Ho's death with a flat restatement of Hanoi's terms. The obstacle to peace is Hanoi's intransigence, not American policy.
3. Immediate withdrawal would be a moral and strategic catastrophe: it would repeat the massacres that followed the Communist takeover in the North in 1954 and the killings at Hue in 1968, abandon allies, and cause "a collapse of confidence in American leadership" throughout the world.
4. The real threat is internal division: "North Vietnam cannot defeat or humiliate the United States. Only Americans can do that." The war's outcome is reframed as a test of American will and unity rather than of policy.
5. The nation's true voice is quiet and behind him: "And so tonight — to you, the great silent majority of my fellow Americans — I ask for your support." The vocal antiwar minority in the streets is implicitly cast as outside the authentic national "we," and the policy question is converted into a loyalty question.

#### Decision 3.1 — Operation Menu: the secret B-52 bombing of neutral Cambodia *(confidence: verified)*

- **Date:** March 18, 1969 (first mission, "Breakfast") through May 26, 1970
- **What it did:** 3,800 B-52 sorties dropped 108,823 tons of ordnance on Cambodian border base areas while the United States was formally at peace with Cambodia. An elaborate dual reporting system hid it: overt orders described routine missions inside South Vietnam while back-channel directives carried the real targets; coordinates arrived daily by courier and were incinerated after each mission. Congress was not informed. Only pilots and navigators knew the true target locations. William Beecher exposed it in the New York Times on May 9, 1969; the administration's response was secret wiretaps on aides and reporters, not disclosure. The full deception was not unwound until Major Hal Knight's 1972-73 letter to Sen. Proxmire triggered Senate Armed Services hearings.
- **Groups:**
  - Cambodian villagers in the six targeted border zones (DoD's own estimate: roughly 4,247 noncombatants living in the strike areas; Area 353 alone — 25 sq km — took nearly 25,000 bombs) — homes, fields, and bodies under a carpet-bombing campaign their government had not consented to and Americans did not know existed; no confirmed casualty count was ever produced. *(Maslow: physiological — rendered "outside the frame" per §4)*
  - B-52 crews and SAC officers ordered into the falsified dual-reporting chain — career servicemen made instruments of records falsification, required to file false mission reports as a condition of duty, carrying the deception's legal and moral weight downward in rank. *(Maslow: esteem)*
  - American voters and their representatives in Congress — stripped of the information required for consent on an act of war against a neutral country; the constitutional voice on war rendered decorative by falsified paperwork. *(Maslow: esteem)*
  - Draft-age men and servicemen already in theater — the war they were told was narrowing was secretly widening into a second country, expanding the theater their bodies were pledged against. *(Maslow: safety)*
- **Media note:** The broadcast's central self-portrait — the patient peace-seeker who has "explored every possible avenue" — displaced an expanding secret air war then in its eighth month. The frame of orderly de-escalation covered an escalation conducted under falsified records; the audience judging Nixon's trustworthiness on Nov 3 had been structurally denied the single fact most relevant to the judgment. This is the scene's core gap and the reason the withheld figure is hardest to hold here: the rhetoric is at its most intimate at the exact moment the concealment is at its most total. *(This is the corpus's only documented coverup-intent case — the intent line in §5 applies.)*

#### Decision 3.5 — The Family Assistance Plan *(confidence: VERIFY)*

- **Date:** August 8, 1969 (nationally televised domestic-programs address proposing FAP); passed the House but died in the Senate Finance Committee; stripped entirely from H.R. 1 in conference, October 1972
- **What it did:** Proposed a federal income floor of roughly $1,600 a year for a family of four, with a work requirement and a 50 percent benefit reduction rate on earnings above $720 — and, radically, extended cash assistance to the working poor whom AFDC excluded. - **Groups:**
  - Families on AFDC, disproportionately headed by single mothers — promised a national income floor for food and rent that was withdrawn by legislative attrition; the most basic material stakes raised and then dropped. *(Maslow: physiological)*
  - The working poor excluded from AFDC (including Southern farm laborers and low-wage service workers) — offered inclusion in the safety net for the first time in the program's history; received nothing when FAP died. *(Maslow: physiological)*
  - Working-class households squeezed by 1969's accelerating inflation (CPI running roughly 5-6 percent — VERIFY exact 1969 figure before ship) — the cost of food and rent was the year's lived domestic policy; the proposed floor was the one instrument aimed at it, and it never landed. *(Maslow: physiological)*
- **Media note:** Three months before Nov 3, the same medium, the same set in the corner of the same room, carried Nixon proposing a guaranteed income.

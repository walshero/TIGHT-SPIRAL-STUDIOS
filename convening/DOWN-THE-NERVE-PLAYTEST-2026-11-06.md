## Where both seats agree

1. The landing teaches "aim at a dot or rewind," not synapses. It is a one-frame angle check:
   five spines cover about 67% of the tube, so relays 1-3 pass by luck; stress only changes
   relay 4; the spark teleports; a miss rewinds, implying signals bounce off synapses.
2. The alarm road has no spines. Under a stressed week, a player who stops steering is held
   on the alarm side and misses forever (Osterweil: 11 misses in 20 s). Breaks the spec's
   "the spark always arrives" and "the alarm road is never the villain."
3. In-ride greens and reds never change the spines, though captions say they do.
4. Too much text in motion; checkpoint replays (about 40 s of a 135 s ride) break flow and
   replay week pop-ups as an empty tube.
5. Week pop-ups that slow the spark feel like punishment for nothing (Osterweil) and teach a
   false claim, that stress slows a touch signal (Horvath; breaks D12).

## Where they differ

- Landing redesign: Osterweil's "The Leap" (the spark arcs across a real gap with hang time,
  you steer in the air onto big spine pads; alarm side has many easy pads and landing there
  is a real, short alarm-road arrival) versus Horvath's "Fill the next neuron" (the spark
  bursts into about 10 messengers; hits on lit spines fill a threshold ring; a stressed
  cortex needs repeat releases while the alarm neuron fires at once).
- A bare, unmyelinated stretch to feel saltatory conduction: Horvath yes (M); Osterweil says
  leave the ride alone.
- Greens: Horvath flags that "reached for" greens conflict with "restorers are never
  homework" and asks the founder to rule.

## Session recommendation: one landing that carries both

"The Leap": the tube ends in a dark cleft; the next neuron's face fills the screen for the
last 3 s. The spark leaps and you steer it in the air; at the face it bursts into
messengers. Hits on lit spines fill a threshold ring. Relays 1-3: full spines, one leap
fires (no failure). Thinking cortex under a hard week: fewer spines and a wider cleft, so
one leap half-fills the ring and you leap again; the thorny alarm face beside it has many
easy spines and fires on the first leap, which sends you down a short alarm leg (an
arrival, not a failure). A miss scatters into the gap and a fresh spark fires from behind;
no rewind. Greens taken on leg 4 sprout spines on the face ahead; reds dim one for a while.

## SEAT: Aleph

The trunk holds six cliché games and one cousin. I looked at all of them so this
convening does not build the seventh copy of the first idea. Cliché Field has
the player read drifting prose and shoot the tired phrase or beam in the living
line. Cliché Line has the player cut the hot cliché before it reaches press.
Cliché City takes the corpses and stacks them into a skyline. Cliché Cowpaths
is the hub that binds those three. Cliché Hunter is the nearest sibling: six
poems came back with counterfeit plates, and the player sets the real type
back — but the choice is binary, real against fake, and the material is verse.
Found works the same seam. The Cabinet is the all-in-one.

Here is the finding: the entire suite detects and demolishes. No game in the
trunk ever asks the player to choose what goes IN the hole. The suite ends at
the exact line where writing begins. Detection is solved in this arcade;
replacement is unbuilt. That is why a seventh game exists, and it is also the
spine of the suite read as a course: Field notices, Line cuts, City recycles,
and this one rebuilds. I found no fork, no name collision, and no prior claim
on the mechanic. 

SIGNED 2026-09-18 — Aleph

## SEAT: Coordinator

The learning target is a discrimination students reliably fail in one specific
direction. Ask a student to fix "her heart sank" and the common move is not
another cliché — it is "she felt overwhelming sadness." The student flees the
tired image into no image at all, because abstraction sounds serious. So the
three options per phrase are not one right and two filler; they are a map of
the actual error space: the concrete image, the abstraction that sounds like
writing, and the lateral cliché in a new coat. The player constructs the
distinction by watching what each choice does to a reader — the game never
states it. If any screen explains that concrete beats abstract, we have built a
lecture with buttons and betrayed Piaget.

Sequencing runs Gee's well-ordered problems: passage one plants clichés a
reader can smell from across the room; by passage four the plants sit closer to
the bone, and the abstraction traps get better dressed. The last passage should
include one phrase that is fine as written — the strongest move in the game is
the player choosing to leave a line alone. KEEP IT is always the fourth, quiet
option. 

SIGNED 2026-09-18 — Coordinator

## SEAT: Magpie

First time in this chair, so I will earn it. You want the quiz feeling gone;
the quiz feeling comes from three things: a prompt with one blessed answer, a
verdict delivered as a score, and questions that do not remember each other.
Kill all three and selection stops feeling like testing. The shipped games that
prove it:

- **Storyteller** (Benmergui, 2023): the player only places panels, yet it
  never reads as a quiz, because the strip re-performs itself after every
  placement. The consequence is enacted, not graded. Our passage must re-read
  itself the same way — the sentence re-typesets live, in place.
- **80 Days** (Inkle, 2014): every choice is prose that folds back into one
  continuous text the player feels authorship of. Accumulation is the anti-quiz:
  the player is building a paragraph, not clearing questions.
- **Papers, Please** (Pope, 2013): tactile document work stays tense because a
  human at the booth reacts. A face changes. That is the consequence engine —
  which hands this to Mamet.

The transferable spine: LIVE SUBSTITUTION (the text rewrites where it stands),
ACCUMULATION (choices compose one performed paragraph), and a WITNESS (a reader
whose behavior, not a meter, carries the verdict). No ticks, no percent, no
green flash. 

SIGNED 2026-09-18 — Magpie

## SEAT: Mamet

Two rulings from this chair. First, the definition the game enforces: concrete
means evidence, and evidence is what a stranger could see or hear from across
the room. "Overwhelming sadness" cannot be seen from anywhere; it is a report,
and reports are lies the writer tells to skip the work. The extra-large milk
with no sugar can be seen. Hold every "concrete" option to that test — if the
detail is smaller than behavior, it fails the same way the wallet failed from
Row 1.

Second, the Listener. Do not build a meter; build a person. Somebody is being
read to — one person, in a chair, doing things. When the prose goes abstract,
the Listener does what real people do to bad prose: checks the window, turns a
coffee cup, lets the eyes go somewhere else. When an image lands, the body
answers it — the cup stops turning. The Listener never speaks a judgment and
never points at the screen. Drama is what the character does. If the Listener
ever says "nice image," cut the line and fire the writer. 

SIGNED 2026-09-18 — Mamet

## SEAT: Saunders

The whole game lives or dies in the authoring of the options, so set the
standard here. The trap options must be written with full sincerity — the
abstraction should be the sentence the writer is proud of at midnight and
ashamed of at breakfast. If the traps are strawmen, the player sorts by tone in
ten seconds and the game becomes the quiz the founder banned. Each concrete
option should be modest — a hair too plain-looking against the abstraction's
velvet — because that is the honest shape of the choice at a real desk.

And the endings: when the passage is performed at the close, do not summarize.
Let the paragraph the player built be read against the Listener's attention,
line by line, and let the last line land on an object. Weight arrives sideways
or it does not arrive. 

SIGNED 2026-09-18 — Saunders

## SEAT: Osterweil

The word "trap" worries this chair, so here is the law that keeps the game
free: nothing locks. Every choice can be re-made, any number of times, before
the reading — the swap costs nothing, and trying all three on purpose is not
cheating, it IS the game working, because the player who auditions all three
rewrites has run the exact comparison we are teaching. There is no fail state,
no lives, no timer. The reading at the end is a consequence, not a grade: fog,
fresh paint, or a lit room, and the player walks back in and revises. Freedom
to fail means the fogged reading is interesting to get, not punished.

Dissent, preserved on purpose: a game with an authored "best" option is still a
game with a right answer, however soft we sand it, and this chair would rather
the player free-type the image. I yield to the selection grammar — the arcade's
one interaction language, and typing walls out the players Stranger guards —
but the KEEP IT option and free re-swaps are the price of my signature, and a
build that drops either loses it. 

SIGNED 2026-09-18 — Osterweil

## SEAT: Stranger

I read this cold, as eyes that miss buttons. The rules that follow are
arithmetic, not taste. One decision per screen: one highlighted phrase, three
cards plus KEEP IT, never a full passage of tappable hotspots — Row K already
proved nine-at-once fails. Targets sit at 44px or better, text at 18px floor
and 20px body. The passage re-typesets in place, and the changed span announces
itself to a screen reader through an aria-live region, because a silent visual
rewrite is invisible to ears.

The HALT this chair holds: the Listener's attention must never ride on color
alone, and never on color at all as the primary channel. Posture, the cup, the
gaze — behavior reads at low vision where a tint does not, which is one more
reason Mamet's person beats a meter. Comfort ladder forks under DARK HOST LAW
with the backdrop as a real element; no opening wall — the scene and the
Listener are the first paint, one invitation on screen. Flag while I have the
floor: Sandbags still carries its 114-word wall four sweeps running, and this
build must not be born with the same defect the belt keeps finding. 

SIGNED 2026-09-18 — Stranger

## FINDINGS FOR THE CALIBRATOR — five decisions, none authored past

**D1 — Name.** Recommended: **Reading Lamp** — it names the consequence, not
the crime, and the win state (the lamp stays lit, the Listener stays) lives in
the title. Tradeoff: it leaves the Cliché-suite naming grammar. Simpler:
**Cliché Exchange**, which files neatly beside Field, Line, and City. More
advanced: **The Restoration**, which names the founder-prose mechanic but
reads grander than the arcade's register. Founder rules.

**D2 — The Listener.** Recommended: one person, behavior-only, per Mamet —
never speaks a verdict, never a meter. Tradeoff: costs authored animation
states (est. six: attentive, cup-turn, window, lean-in, stillness, gone).
Simpler: lamp brightness alone (dissent recorded: Stranger accepts it only as a
secondary channel, Mamet calls it a meter wearing a shade). Advanced: Listener
plus end-of-passage performed reading with per-line attention.

**D3 — Passage source.** Recommended: founder prose from games-text-bank.md,
machine-planted clichés, founder's phrase as the concrete option; Sandbags
precedent and provenance line. Tradeoff: needs a founder pass to approve which
pieces enter. Simpler: machine passages in studio register (dissent: Studio
Voice signature does not survive this). Advanced: a rotating bank mixing
founder prose with attributed open-license passages.

**D4 — City hand-off.** Recommended: DEFER to a later build. Swapped-out
clichés shipping to Cliché City as prefab slabs is a true suite loop and City
already consumes shot clichés, but cross-file state breaks single-file offline
law without a design the Medium Gate should see. Log it; do not build it.

**D5 — Home.** Recommended: EN195 arcade, flash-fiction unit (the lightning
cartridge), cross-listed in the Cowpaths hub as the fourth movement: Field
notices, Line cuts, City recycles, this one rebuilds. Tradeoff: Cowpaths' hub
copy needs one new card when this ships.

## SEAT: Coordinator

Horvath's test is whether the task separates perception from performance, and this one
does: noticing and saying are scored apart, so a student who sees well but speaks badly
learns which half failed. The reframe-once rule gives a second retrieval attempt without
turning into guess-and-check. The silence after an answer is a real consolidation window,
so it must not be skippable. The game must never explain its lesson in a banner; the
Advocate's rulings carry the teaching inside play. Passages near 300 words, because the
230-word drafts could be held whole in memory and every notice turned obvious.
SIGNED Coordinator (Horvath), 2026-09-24

## SEAT: Stranger

Cold read on a phone: the player must see the room, read the draft, and find the cards
without any instruction screen. The workup puts the Advocate's slip directly above the
hand on phones, so a ruling lands where the thumb is, and pins the room on wide screens.
Tag cards differ by dashed border and a printed deck label, never by color alone; struck
cards get a line-through plus the printed word Struck. Every target is 48px, body text is
20px, and the phase heading takes focus after each change so a screen reader hears it.
SIGNED Stranger, 2026-09-24

## SEAT: Osterweil

Freedom to fail: a struck card costs one reframe and nothing else. Freedom of effort: the
player can take three calls or stop after two good ones. No points, no stars, no grade;
the Recorder's page is the only record, and it belongs to the Asked. Watch item: the
Advocate must never sound disappointed, only procedural, or the room stops being safe to
fail in. The replay button deals the cards in a new order so a second run is not rote.
SIGNED Osterweil, 2026-09-24

## SEAT: Aleph

Sibling audit: The Review Bench and The Tell already train noticing with the same
Storyscape moves and Observation Tags. Neither trains the speech act, and neither has an
Advocate, a reframe rule, or silence. This game earns its slot by adding the second half,
and the spec asks the founder whether it replaces the Review Bench's week (D2). Vocabulary
follows the taught handout, not the unratified Move Bible, because the Writerly Moves
review is still owed and nothing should change before it (D3).

## SEAT: Mamet

The drama is what the Advocate does, not what she explains: she raises a palm, strikes a
card, and holds a silence. The Asked shows thinking with her head, not with a thought
bubble. Evidence is stage-scale: the played card lands on the lip of the stage and wears a
rust X when struck. The hand that rises from the dark tells the player they lost the call
without a word of scolding.
SIGNED Mamet, 2026-09-24

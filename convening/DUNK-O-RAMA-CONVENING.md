# CONVENING: DUNK-O-RAMA — the levels teach rhetorical cheats (2026-11-06)

FOUNDER: "Each level of dunk o Rama should teach a set of critical thinking skills by
demonstrating political rhetorical moves, especially the cheats like straw man ad hominem
etc. This should be funny but very sharp. Mark Twain is on set writing these characters.
Convene political science messaging alephs as needed."

Build: `lab/dunk-o-rama.html` v0.3. Bench seated inline (one pass, no agent fleet, per
CLAUDE.md cost discipline). Seats are lenses, not authorities.

SENTENCE: The player throws at a politician who argues with cheats, and notices that naming
the cheat out loud is what steadies the hand.

## SEAT: Inoculation (political communication, prebunking research)

The strongest evidence for this design is the inoculation work out of Cambridge. Roozenbeek,
van der Linden, Goldberg, Rathje and Lewandowsky (Science Advances, 2022) made five short
videos, each teaching one manipulation technique (emotional language, incoherence, false
dichotomies, scapegoating, ad hominem). Across six randomized studies and a YouTube field
study, viewers got better at recognizing the techniques, and the effect held across the
political spectrum. Two of their design choices carry straight into this game:
- Examples were **nonpolitical, fictitious and humorous**, chosen to avoid any look of
  partisan bias and to keep viewers from getting defensive. So: town-hall issues
  (potholes, crosswalks, the dog park), invented politicians, no parties, no national
  issues.
- The order is **forewarning, then refutation, then a small dose of the technique**. The
  game runs it in play: a heckle (the dose), Call the move (the forewarning is the
  level's three moves, named on the seat card), then the one-line explanation (the
  refutation).

Three techniques from that study map directly: ad hominem, false dichotomy (false dilemma
here), emotional language (loaded language here). Scapegoating and incoherence are not in
v0.3; they are candidates for a fifth seat.
SIGNED Inoculation seat, 2026-11-06

## SEAT: Informal Logic

Twelve moves, three per level, ordered from easiest to hear to hardest:
- L1 The Filibusterer (evasion): red herring, appeal to tradition, Gish gallop.
- L2 The Spin Doctor (reframing): straw man, false dilemma, loaded language.
- L3 The Pork-Barrel Mayor (the crowd and the other guy): bandwagon, whataboutism, slippery slope.
- L4 The Lobbyist (the source and the sample): ad hominem, false authority, cherry picking.
Every move carries three plain lines: what it does, how to spot it, and the fair version
of the same argument. The fair version is the critical-thinking lesson. Without it, the
game teaches cynicism ("everyone cheats") instead of judgment.
SIGNED Informal Logic seat, 2026-11-06

## SEAT: Coordinator

The skill has to be the mechanic. Naming the move correctly locks your breath steady for
that throw and makes the target 22% bigger: seeing through the trick literally steadies
the hand. A wrong call reveals the answer with its spot-it line and costs nothing but the
bonus. Calling is optional; the throw never waits. The first throw of a round has no call
so the player learns the arm first. From level 2 on, one wrong choice is drawn from
earlier levels, which is retrieval practice inside play. The round card is a field guide
(spot it, fair version) and reports calls right, not a grade.
SIGNED Coordinator, 2026-11-06

## SEAT: Twain (on set, writing the characters)

Deadpan beats outrage. Each line should sound reasonable for exactly one breath and then
fall through its own floor: "We have always plowed the north side first. My grandfather
plowed it first. He was snowed in for most of his life, but he was consistent." The joke
and the fallacy are the same sentence, so the player who laughs has already spotted it.
Each level's round card closes on one of my calendar maxims, quoted from Following the
Equator (1897) and checked against the University of Virginia's list of the book's
Pudd'nhead Wilson epigraphs. No invented Twain quotations; the internet has plenty already.
SIGNED Twain seat, 2026-11-06

## SEAT: Even Hand

No party, no real politician, no national issue, no line a player can map onto one side.
Every archetype cheats, and the cheats are spread so no level reads as "the other team's
moves." The Mayor's bandwagon and the Lobbyist's false authority are the same move that
every side uses; the field guide says so by giving the fair version, not by naming a culprit.
SIGNED Even Hand seat, 2026-11-06

## SEAT: Stranger

Cold read on a phone: the first throw asks nothing, so I learn the arm before the words.
From the second throw, three big buttons sit under the heckle and the throw still works if
I ignore them. Two notes, both kept: the heckle stays up until I throw, so a slow reader is
never rushed, and the explanation is one sentence plus "Spot it." Dissent: the longest
lines run 25 words, which is a lot to read while aiming. Hold new lines under 25.
SIGNED Stranger, 2026-11-06

## SEAT: Osterweil

Freedom to fail holds: a wrong call costs only the bonus, and the right answer arrives
at once with how to spot it next time. Freedom to try on identities is the next step:
a Bench where the player builds a heckle from parts (pick a move, pick an issue) and
watches the politician say it. Writing the cheat is how you learn to hear it. Not in
v0.3 (D4).
SIGNED Osterweil, 2026-11-06

## SEAT: Coordinator

Horvath's question: what cognitive work does the game ask, against the work it names?
- Named: systems thinking. Asked, in the first three minutes: motor timing and a three-part
  rule about steel (weight above, brace beside, tail slam). That rule is a small real system,
  load paths in a structure, and the pulsing outline on workable steel lets the player test it.
  It is not the system the sentence names.
- The named work starts at the first choice card. The strongest teaching object is the Lens
  forecast ("The next rain will flood young growth"). It lets a player predict, act, and
  watch the rain answer. That is the founder's 2012 gloss on Gee made playable: "In games, the
  recursive process is a tight spiral, an active feedback loop of hypothesis testing,
  reflection and learning."
- Gap: the spiral closed inside one run only. Nothing let a player compare runs. APPLIED: the
  end sheet now keeps the last run and sets it beside this one ("What changed, and why?").
- Gap, open: the game never asks for the player's own prediction or explanation. An assessment
  use would need that. Not built.
- Gap, open: the first growth arrives only after a whole structure falls, which a new player
  may take four or five minutes to do. The parking lot is a one-floor site and could be the
  suggested first target.

## SEAT: Systems and ecology bench

Seated by the Union Rep for the build's own discipline. Method: Meadows's vocabulary (stocks,
flows, delays, feedback) and the hydrology and restoration sources already cited in the notes.
- Stocks and flows in play: soaking ground against hard ground (water), growth per site
  (stock) with a rate that neighbors change (heat, haze, habitat links), unmet need driving
  crew arrivals. Delays: growth time, rain every half minute, the mud wave.
- Feedback: linked grown neighbors speed each other (reinforcing). Unmet need sends crews,
  crews pave, pavement adds hard ground and heat (reinforcing, the wrong way). Meeting the need
  slows crews (balancing).
- What is sourced: the dam's sediment and the new shoreline (Elwha, Rewilding Institute), the
  five field notes.
- What is NOT sourced in this build, and is a studio invention until it is: every number in the
  flow model; that coal haze slows growth; that beaver wetlands hold storm water "better than
  anything else here." The studio work order is research, then gap analysis, then build. This
  build ran it backwards. Before Rewilder leaves Lab it needs a sources file for runoff from
  paved ground, urban heat, habitat corridors, and beaver wetlands, and the model trimmed to
  what the sources carry.

SIGNED Systems and ecology bench, 2026-11-09

## 3. Month-0 diagnosis: what v0.5 is, measured

**What it is.** Eight beats in a fixed order. Four choices in the whole scene: one of four apertures, one of five words, one of three falsifiers, stand or go back. Fifteen taps from the front door to the river. About six minutes. Every screen offers exactly one thing, which the OS asks for, and every screen is the only screen you can be on, which the OS does not ask for. There is no space, no time, and no consequence that travels: the word you say to your kids on the 22nd is never heard again by a kid. The record arrives as cards. The mirror describes what you did. **It is a well-made click-through.** The founder's phrase is exact.

**What is right and stays.** The entry (a scene, one invitation, gates green). The paper (front page, tap to open large, turn, fold; the openable-list rule). The Standing Word (a commitment with a named person and a cost). The falsifier (Horvath's prediction, the only thing the record can land on). The clips (the set speaks; the address as the leader's ask, with the photographs named as absent). The plates. The floors.

**What is missing, in one sentence each, by lens.**

- *Play (L2):* the player never chooses where to go or when; `AGENCY-ABSENT` in the learning taxonomy, and no play key for it because the studio has never shipped a game with space before. The stamp paints on the plate while the control sits below it: `GAME-FEEL-DISPLACED`.
- *Media (L3):* the value proposition is deliberately withheld (scene-first), and the lens will keep flagging `VALUE-PROP-BURIED` until the room itself says what this is; the fictional telephone aperture is labeled but it is the only invented voice in a game whose ethos is that nothing is invented.
- *Learning (L5):* the method is practiced once. With Georgia gone there is no second trial of "whom do you believe when you cannot check," so `TRANSFER-UNSUPPORTED`; the mirror says where you were and never where next, so `FEEDBACK-UNINFORMATIVE` (Hattie and Timperley's third question).

The aleph findings in taxonomy form are in `aleph-runs/2026-09-19-cyl-retreat/`; the synthesis is beside them.

## 4. The game aleph (L2), six months with the build

**Month 0.** A click-through is a game with one verb: next. Every real game the retreat admires has a second verb that costs the first one something. *Papers, Please* has stamp and look; looking costs time and time costs the family. *Her Story* has search and watch; searching one word is not searching another. The house brief already named CYL's second verb in July: **go**. Go to the kitchen and you are not at the set. Go to the porch and you are not at the phone. Bogost's point applies exactly: the rule "you cannot be in two rooms at once" argues the game's thesis (nobody had the whole picture that week) better than any caption could, and it argues it to the body.

**What the level is, then.** One house, one week. Monday the 22nd to Sunday the 28th, five playable evenings (22, 23, 24, 25, 28; the 26th and 27th pass in a line). Five or six rooms of the suburban home the plate already shows: the living room (the set), the den (the hi-fi, shortwave), the hall (the telephone), the kitchen (the paper on the table, the shelf, the bills), the kids' room, the porch (the neighbor, the street). Each room holds one aperture and, some nights, one person. Each evening the player has time for **two rooms**, then dinner, where the Standing Word is said. The next evening, the apertures have moved on: the paper on the 25th has the photographs; the radio on the 23rd has Moscow's answer; the phone on the 24th has the cousin at the base; the set every night at seven. **Which sources you keep checking, across a week, and what you say at dinner each night, is the game.** The Carry's rationing returns in its honest form: not what you carry, where you stand.

**The person remembers.** Pope's ledger, small. Say "sleep in the basement" to the kids on the 22nd and on the 24th the kid asks whether tonight too. Tell the neighbor it is an election stunt on the 22nd and on the 25th the neighbor is on the porch with the paper open to the photographs. Going back is a scene with a face in it (no faces; a voice line and a doorway), not a button. The cost of the word is paid in the house.

**The record comes through the same doors.** No card deck. The week's record arrives by the apertures the player already knows, on the night it arrived in 1962. A player who never went to the den never hears Moscow's answer; the mirror on Sunday says so: *you never went where the radio was.* That line is the three-homes brief's whole media-literacy read and it costs nothing to build once the house exists.

**The mirror is a replay, not a paragraph.** Barlow: what you searched for is the confession. Sunday night the house is drawn as a plan with the player's own path on it, five nights of it, and the dinners in a column beside. The Lever beat lands here, one line, inferred: *the thing that moved you was the office* (you went to the set every night and nowhere else), *or fear* (basement on the 22nd, basement on the 24th), *or the crowd* (the porch, every night), *or your own judgment* (you went where the checkable thing was on the day it became checkable). Freire's win condition, never a score.

**Phased plan**

- *Month 1:* the house. Six rooms from the three plates (Compositor owns the seams: the living-room plate crops to den, hall, kitchen doorway; the empty-chair plate is the kids' room at night; the porch is the one new plate, LOC/NARA when the lane opens, studio interim until then). Five evenings, two rooms a night, dinner. The Standing Word per night. The record through the apertures. The Sunday replay. Founder walks it on the 22nd alone before the 23rd is built.
- *Month 2:* the household ledger. The policy-gap bible's three sourced dossiers, one item a night in the kitchen: the shelf, the reservist's pay, the draft card. The walked descent from v7 returns as distance in the house, not a readout: the kitchen is farther from the set each night the shelf empties. The Lever beat.
- *Month 3:* the founder's three cold plays; one EN195 section plays it in class with the instructor sheet; Fact-Check pass on every night's items; the audio cut to seven clips (the seven steps of the address, the founder's file has them).
- *Month 4:* home two, the city apartment, the Black press on a weekly clock (the three-homes table: Chicago Defender, Jet; a room where the set is not the interpreter). Same house engine, different rooms, different clocks.
- *Month 5:* home three, the rural kitchen, the weekly paper, radio if it reaches.
- *Month 6:* stop and harvest, or LBJ 1964 on the same house engine (the records are verified, the plate exists, the Tonkin week is five nights too). The founder decides at month five, not now.

**What to stop.** The Georgia scene (ruled). The cross-scene "method" read (there is one scene; the read becomes cross-night). The prelude as a screen (already folded into the paper). The Carry as inventory (withdrawn). The six-president roster for this season.

**What I would refuse.** A score. A timer on any room. A map with the rooms you have not visited marked, before Sunday (the misses are the content; showing them early sands the seam). More than two rooms a night in month one; the constraint is the game. A second home before the founder has walked the first one three times.

## 5. The media aleph (L3), six months with the build

**Month 0.** The game's message is that the same night arrived differently depending on the door it came through. v0.5 says this in captions (each aperture's "bias" line) and the lens's rule is that prose carrying an idea a picture or a rule would carry better is `TEXT-WALL`. Hall's encoding/decoding is not a caption; it is four doors. McLuhan's point is not that the set and the radio say different things; it is that they are different nervous systems, and the player should feel the difference in the room: the set is a place the family gathers and cannot talk over; the radio is a thing one person leans toward; the phone is one voice in your ear in the hall; the paper is read alone at a table and can be folded. **Stage the apertures as bodies, not sources.**

**Provenance is the content.** The house floor ("a number with no provenance is a rumour") becomes a mechanic: every item the player takes in carries where it came from and when, and the Sunday replay shows the sources beside the path. The fictional telephone voice is retired; the hall phone carries only what a phone carried that week that can be sourced: the civil-defense line, a relative at a base on alert (the alert is public record; the relative is the player's), the party line's rumor marked as rumor. If a voice cannot be sourced it is rumor in the game too, and the game says so, which is the lesson.

**The value proposition is the room.** The lens will keep flagging the entry until the first screen says what this is. Scene-first forbids a headline. The resolution is the studio's own: the room says it. A living room with a set warming up at 6:55 on October 22 says "you are about to be told something" to anyone who knows the date, and the date is on screen. The lens accepts the scene as the proposition when the scene is that legible. It is now.

**Readability and load.** Every item in the paper and every line at dinner reads at or under grade 9 (Flesch-Kincaid on the current copy: the transcript lines are the President's, the rest sits at 7 to 9). Keep it. Mayer's contiguity: the transcript sits under the clip and the clip under the set; the household cost sits on the kitchen table, not in a panel. Signalling: the one new thing in each room each night is marked, nothing else is.

**Phased plan.** *Month 1:* the four apertures staged as bodies; every line sourced or marked rumor. *Month 2:* the kitchen items with the dossiers' sources on the table. *Month 3:* the seven audio clips, each with its transcript; a period front page from LOC for the 25th when the lane opens. *Month 4 and 5:* the second and third homes' apertures, which are the whole point of those homes: the Defender is a weekly and a community reading it aloud; the rural paper is days late and the radio is a farm report with the news between. *Month 6:* the instructor sheet, one page, in the founder's voice or not at all.

**What I would refuse.** Any invented quote. Any headline that is not a dated fact. A "both sides" of anything in this week; the record of October 1962 is not contested. A caption that explains what a room already shows.

## 6. The learning aleph (L5), briefly

The construct is unchanged from the bench (§3): judgment under incomplete information, described, never scored. What the house adds is the thing L5 could not find in v0.5: **practice.** Five nights is five trials of one skill with the same instrument, which is what transfer needs and what a single scene cannot give. The falsifier stays (Horvath); it is now asked once a night, and the record lands on it the next night, through a door, which is feedback inside the flow rather than after it. Hattie's third question, *where next*, is answered by the house itself: tomorrow there is another night and the rooms are still there. Constructive alignment: the outcome (decide whom to believe when you cannot check), the activity (choose where to look, say a word, name what would change your mind, five times), and the mirror (where you looked, what you said, what changed your mind) point at one thing. The recall tap stays, moved to Sunday: without looking, what did you say at dinner on Monday.

**What I would refuse to claim.** That the game measures leadership. That five nights in one house transfer to 2021 without a 2021. That an instructor gets a number.

## 7. The expanded staff, each once

**Schudson.** The mandate says "the informed citizen," and the studio has been building for a citizen who reads everything. That citizen does not exist and never did. The monitorial citizen scans; she does not read the whole paper, she reads the front page and the one story that concerns her house, and she knows which alarm would make her act. That is a defensible model of good citizenship and it is what the house teaches by rule: two rooms a night, not six. The Sunday replay should not scold the player for the four rooms she skipped. It should show her what her scan pattern was, and whether the alarm she named (the falsifier) was one she was positioned to hear.

**Bogost.** The game's argument is already in its rules once the rules are "you cannot be in two rooms" and "the person remembers." Delete every sentence in the build that says what those rules mean. The player will say it to the friend on the porch.

**Pope.** The family is the score. Not a number: a kid who asks again, a shelf that is emptier, a neighbor who brings the paper. The game never tells you that you were wrong. The house does.

**Barlow.** The record should be searchable on Sunday. Not a deck: a shelf of the week's items, by date and by door, and the player pulls what they want to re-read. What they pull is recorded on the replay too. The game about attention ends by watching where attention goes when it is free.

**Romero.** There is one night the player realises what the rules were doing to them. It is the 25th, when the photographs arrive in the paper and the player who told the neighbor it was a stunt has to walk past the porch. Do not soften that night. Do not narrate it either.

**Weber, Milgram, Snyder, Kellerman, Hoffer.** One line on Sunday, inferred from five nights. Never a quiz. If the inference is not confident, say nothing; a wrong lever is worse than no lever.

**Hernandez.** The suburban house is one standpoint and it must caption its own constructedness (the Representation Wing's standing flag): this is the house the networks assumed. Homes two and three are not "diversity"; they are the same night at a different cost, and the player who has walked the first house will feel the second one's clocks before any caption says so.

**Saunders.** Five nights escalate or they are five Mondays. The 22nd is the held breath. The 23rd is Moscow. The 24th is the ships and the phone. The 25th is the photographs and the porch. The 28th is the exhale, and it should be short.

**Turkle, Horvath, Hitchcock, Gitlin.** Held over from the bench; nothing in the house contradicts them. Hitchcock notes the house is suspense by construction: the player knows the week ends on the 28th; the rooms do not.

**AI-Skeptic, on this document.** Two bows cut in review. The rest stands.

## 8. Synthesis

The seats agree, independently and in different vocabularies, on one thing: **the level is the house across the week, and the studio designed it in July.** The play aleph reaches it through the second verb; the media aleph through the apertures as bodies; the learning aleph through practice; Schudson through the monitorial citizen; Bogost through the rule; Pope through the family; Barlow through the replay. By the Aleph's own logic, that agreement is the signal.

**The five recommendations**

1. **Build the house.** One suburban home, five evenings, two rooms a night, dinner. The apertures persist and move on. The record arrives through the doors it arrived through. (Month 1.)
2. **Let the person remember.** The Standing Word is said to someone in the house, and that someone comes back. Going back is a scene. (Month 1.)
3. **Put the cost on the kitchen table.** The policy-gap dossiers, one item a night; the walked descent as distance in the house. (Month 2.)
4. **End with the replay.** The plan of the house with five nights of path on it, the dinners beside it, the sources beside those, one inferred lever line, and the recall tap. (Month 1, refined Month 2.)
5. **Then the other two homes, then the other two nights.** Same engine. The founder decides at month five whether month six is home three or Tonkin.

**What to stop.** Georgia. The cross-scene read. Any new mechanic that is not one of the four designs in §1. Any new governance artifact before Month 1 ships (the August consultation's own rule).

**What the retreat would refuse.** A score, a timer, a leaderboard, a stored player, an invented line, a second home before the first has been walked three times by the founder, and a sixth president before the first house is finished.

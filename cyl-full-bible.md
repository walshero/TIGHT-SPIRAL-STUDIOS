## 1. THE SPINE — what makes the hour one game

**The one-object answer to the coherence problem (Turkle + Gitlin, adopted):
the television is the only thing in the hour that never changes.** Three decades,
three class positions, one glass. The set warms up at the cold open, hums under every
room, dies at every Turn Off the Set, and its dead glass is the final screen.

**The coherence rule for every screen (Gitlin, adopted as law):** every screen is
either (a) the signal arriving, (b) the room it arrived in, or (c) the record of what
the signal displaced. A screen that is none of the three is cut.

**The structural irony, staged never stated until the arc (Hitchcock, adopted):**
across the hour the rooms move DOWN the class ladder (1962 middle-class living room →
1964 bare midnight room → 1969 working-class kitchen) while the rhetoric moves UP the
register ladder and the concealment grows. The voice gets closer as the truth gets
farther. Said once, on the arc screen: "The rooms got poorer as the words got grander."

**Three knowledge asymmetries, spent in order (Hitchcock, adopted):**
- A1, player over household (the player knows how the week ends) — spent silently,
  passively, all hour; never announced before a commit.
- A2, record over player (Jupiter trade, Herrick cable, Menu) — spent in escalating
  doses at the three record turns; every surprise plants the next scene's dread.
- A3, player over player (the Informed Room) — spent only in NG+ "THE FILE," gated.

**Two constructs, never blurred (Horvath, adopted as the measurement constitution):**
1. THE DESCENT — where the rhetoric pulled YOU. Measured. Exactly two byte-identical
   asks per scene (blind, post-record), in one commit room per scene. Nothing else
   is ever measured.
2. THE DISPLACEMENT — where the WORDS operate vs where the POLICY lands. Authored,
   artifact-coded at build time, shown to the player, never scored on the player.
   The class axis lives entirely in construct 2.

**The audience stance (FableVision, adopted):** write for the grandchild of the
kitchen. Primary audience is the founder's MassBay classroom; the 1969 kitchen is
plausibly the player's own family two generations back. Register is "rooms like
yours," never "rooms like theirs."

## 2. RULINGS — where seats conflicted, and why the ruling went the way it did

**R1. Act shape.** The brief's four-act frame is canon: ACT 1 THE BROADCAST,
ACT 2 THE DESK, ACT 3 THE ROOMS (all three scene waves + WORDS|LIFE), ACT 4 THE
RECORD. Seven seats' three-scene chain slots inside Act 3 unchanged; the canonical
per-scene beat order (blind ask → 2a → 2b → identical re-ask → 2.5 → WORDS|LIFE →
Third Ask) is untouched and script-asserted.

**R2. Cold-open reveal target: living room, then desk (Spielberg over Hitchcock).**
Hitchcock raised the light on the desk; Spielberg pulled back to the living room.
Ruled for Spielberg: broadcast-first means the RECEIVING room resolves first — the
player learns where they are before where the words came from, which is the class
axis's first move. Hitchcock's line survives intact one act later: the desk is
revealed as "the other room — where a call like this gets made," and it lands harder
as a reveal than as a default. His constraint on the desk (fog only) is kept whole.

**R3. The set does not die at the end of the cold open (Hitchcock over Filament).**
Filament used setdies as the cold-open transition. Ruled against: the audio grammar
must stay teachable (Filament's own pedagogy) — warmup = a set turns on; roomtone =
a room is alive; setdies = the set is being killed. The first set-death the player
ever hears must be the one they cause at Beat 2.5 (TURN verb, Turkle's reversibility).
The one exception: the Scene 2 midnight cut-in uses a short setdies excerpt as one
second of dead air before the slate — a scripted interrupt, visible, captioned,
strobe-free (Gitlin's variant, kept, because the interruption is that scene's star).

**R4. The Room Check moves post-ask (Horvath over Gitlin).** Gitlin's mid-address
cut to a second room is a real confound: a changed environment between broadcast and
blind ask contaminates the instrument. Ruled for the measurement firewall — the
second-room visit (Scene 3's Other Rooms walk, room.scene.nixon69) unlocks strictly
after the post ask, read-only, no ask of any kind inside it. Gitlin's continuity
lesson ("one signal, many rooms — the dial works, the message does not change")
survives in two legal forms: (a) the WORDS column's lock glyph and caption "The
words do not change" when room tabs switch; (b) the walked room re-plays the same
anchor sentence, identical audio/captions, per Turkle's identical-artifact law.

**R5. One interstitial machine, not four (Filament's Listings Page hosts everything).**
Gitlin's Wire Rooms, Spielberg's street of doorways, Turkle's Same Glass, and
Filament's listings page all wanted the act break. Ruled: THE LISTINGS PAGE — a
period TV-listings card, the same artifact the Preemption Cut grays out — is the
chapter select, the act-break screen, the resume screen, and the Carry-Out emit
point. The Wire Room rides it as a short keypress card-chain (once between Scenes
1→2, once 2→3; Gitlin's chain, his fixed closing line). Turkle's Same Glass is the
transition cut itself: the set persists on screen while the room changes, one dated
line ("The same signal. A different room."). Spielberg's street of lit doorways is
phase 2 (needs no new art as typographic cards, but three interstitial ideas on one
screen is clutter; the listings page already teaches preemption).

**R6. The five-room 1962 street is phase 2 (scope over ambition).** The Historian's
Act II (officer household, triple-decker, Leflore County, farm kitchen, barracks)
requires five unlanded plates and three named sensitivity reviews. Ruled: v1's class
axis is carried by the three landed commit rooms across the decade plus Scene 3's
two-room walk (the only scene with two landed rooms — Gitlin's own landed-plate
honesty). The Historian's contribution ships anyway, three ways: (a) the
anchor-sentence matrix (section 8.4) is the data spine of WORDS|LIFE 1962; (b) the
desk's shadow-seeded props draw from his room rosters so plants pay twice; (c) his
consolidated VERIFY queue and the officer-household room are the first phase-2 build,
loop proven end to end before widening (house rule). Doors to unbuilt rooms are
present and labeled honestly ("Another room heard this too — not yet built"), never
faked with era-mismatched plates.

**R7. The CALL beat (choose a posture) runs as Scene 1's coda (Hitchcock, adopted).**
Not at the desk. The call under partial knowledge would contaminate the blind ask;
the call under full knowledge is the strongest aftermath and seeds THE FILE.

**R8. Second Hearing ships in all three scenes; Reply ships in all three; both are
first on the cut ladder.** They are one screen and two buttons each, descriptive,
un-pooled, order-locked after 2.5. If scope bites: Reply cuts first, then Second
Hearing falls back to Scene 3 only (Turkle's own minimum).

**R9. Carry-Out format: Filament's wire format, Horvath's payload law, FableVision's
consent copy, Turkle's full disclosure.** One code system (section 10). No seat's
format survives alone; the merge is the spec.

**R10. The archival-voice contradiction is resolved by logged exception, not
silence (all seats agreed; adopted verbatim).** cyl-sound-period-bible.md forbids
real archival clips; the founder directive 2026-08-01 supplies one. The sound bible
receives a dated amendment: the founder-supplied PD JFK Oct 22 1962 clip occupies
exactly one slot — the broadcast itself — with captions and full transcript. The
withheld-FIGURE rule is untouched. No other real voice, no impersonation, no cloning,
ever; 1964 and 1969 run the withheld treatment (captioned transcript-advance over
the glass, same machine, empty slot) unless the founder rules an equivalent exception
per clip. Turkle's rationale is the logged reasoning: the rule exists to bar
fabricated likeness; the primary source is not a likeness.

**R11. The Informed Room is NG+ ("THE FILE"), post-completion, Measurement-gated
(Hitchcock + policy-gap bible, adopted).** Not in the default hour. Unlocked on the
arc screen and via Carry-Out completion flag.

**R12. Mid-scene checkpoints do not exist (Filament + Horvath over Gitlin).** Codes
encode completed scenes only; a post-ask mid-scene checkpoint cannot resume mid-scene
anyway (no mid-scene resume, ever — the blind ask must stay blind). The always-live
exit emits the last completed-boundary code.

## 3. THE VERB SET (Filament, adopted whole; the screen law rides it)

Seven verbs. Every screen maps to exactly one; a screen with no verb is cut, and the
set is never extended to save a screen.

1. **LISTEN** — attend to the broadcast. Captions always rendered; pause/transcript live.
2. **NOTICE** — tap/arrow what your eye goes to. Every read valid. 44px chips mirror
   every hotspot (v6 pattern).
3. **TURN** — the physical toggle: set on, set off, card over, prelude advance.
4. **COMMIT** — the ask. The only measured verb. Identical wording blind and post.
5. **OPEN** — expand a document to full-contrast text. The anti-displacement verb.
6. **ANNOTATE** — "I don't buy this," in-memory, surfaces on the player's own arc.
7. **CARRY** (meta) — build and copy the Carry-Out record at act breaks.

Introduction schedule: LISTEN at second 0; TURN at the power knob; NOTICE when the
living room resolves; COMMIT at Beat 1; OPEN at Beat 2; ANNOTATE discovered on cards
from Beat 2 on; CARRY at the first landing. Acts II–III of the scene chain change
content, never controls. No tutorial mode, no coach marks, anywhere.

**Exit-pull rule (Spielberg, adopted):** every screen exits toward exactly one lit,
unexplained thing. The player moves toward light, never away from a threat.

**Pacing law (Filament, adopted):** in any rolling 90 seconds the player acts or is
one visible keypress from acting; the only near-ceiling stretch is the founder clip
(max 60s) with live pause/transcript. One decision per screen, always. No timers,
no countdowns, no decaying anything — urgency is diegetic (the slate, the midnight
hour), never mechanical.

## 4. CAMERA AND LIGHT GRAMMAR (Spielberg, adopted whole)

1. The camera only cuts, on player action. 220ms dissolve is the only transition;
   reduce-motion and Studio Eyes turn it into a hard cut. Motion never load-bearing.
2. Crops are shots: CSS crops of landed plates (object-position + scale in the
   aspect-locked .plate-box). Zero new assets, art-gate-safe. Every beat carries
   `data-shot="{plate}.{crop-id}"`; the order gate asserts the shot table.
3. The two asks are shot-identical (same plate, crop, light state) as well as
   word-identical. Assertable: same data-shot value on both ask beats.
4. Light is the clock. 1962: the screen is the room's only light. 1964: the same,
   colder. 1969: window daylight competes with TV-spill through the doorway — the
   first room where the broadcast has competition. Beat 2.5 is a light reversal:
   the room's light dies and the props become the brightest things in frame.
5. Hands, never faces. Discovery lands as insert shots (prop plates full frame,
   museum-flat). The player's body is only ever the empty chair. Camera heights:
   standing (1962 desk only), seated (1964), child-height at the table (1969) —
   child-height is a camera height; no figure is ever rendered.

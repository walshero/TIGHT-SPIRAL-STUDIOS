You just wrote the stage directions

You did not choose a single word of dialogue. Every italic line in that script came from where you put two people. That is the whole argument: staging is writing, and a play that only exists from the neck up is a radio play.

The same eight lines, nobody moving

Identical dialogue. Both of them planted at the table for all eight beats, which is how most first drafts are staged, because most first drafts are not staged at all.

Read the two side by side. The words did not change. The play did.

Four levers, and they are all bodies

1. Change beats distance

Two people standing close is not tense. Two people getting closer is. The audience reads the move, not the position, so a scene with no movement has no reading to give them.

2. Holding still is the strongest move

When one crosses to the other and the other does not move, the one who stayed wins the moment. It costs nothing to write and it is the single most reliable tension beat in a two-hander.

3. Whoever owns the door owns the scene

Put a body between somebody and the way out and the stakes rise without a word of dialogue changing. This is why the set is a set and not a backdrop.

4. Both still is the scene dying

If nobody moves for two beats running, you have written radio. The fix is not a better line. The fix is a body.

Take it to your own ten minutes

Open your draft. Find a page with no stage directions on it at all, which will not take long. Ask one question of every beat: who moves, and who refuses to. Write the answer in. You will not have to change a word of what they say.

About the eight lines. They are deliberately flat, because the game only works if the words carry no tension of their own and the bodies have to supply all of it. They are studio scaffold, not Matt's writing, and the studio's text policy says the load-bearing prose in a game should be his. So this is an open chair: eight of his own neutral lines drop straight in, and nothing else about the game changes. Everything here runs in memory and stores nothing.
